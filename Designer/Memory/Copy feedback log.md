## 1. No staccato "2-4 word line. Full stop. New line." construction
- What went wrong: Copy was full of short symmetrical beats like "Smart people consume more. Wise people consume less." and "More podcasts. More threads. More input." Choppy fragments stacked on their own lines.
- Why (user's words): "still 2-4 word lines then a full stop and then a new line... it doesn't even feel human."
- How to apply: Write in flowing conversational sentences, the way the account owner actually talks. Asymmetry, not balanced two-beat contrast pairs. A contrasting pair as a formula ("The content was fine. The format was the problem.") is a banned structure. Vary sentence length hard. Let lines breathe as real spoken thoughts, not slogans.

## 2. The cover hook must trigger disbelief, not state a clean aphorism
- What went wrong: Cover "Smart people consume more. Wise people consume less." was a tidy aphorism. Not compelling. Reader nods and moves on.
- Why (user's words): "It needs to be something that people would look with their eyes opened and say how, why, this can't be possible and then the entire slides would push that through and make them understand and answer their questions."
- How to apply: The cover hook is a curiosity gap / near-impossible claim that makes the reader go "how is that even possible?" Every following slide exists to answer that question and pay off the gap. Build the whole carousel as the answer to the hook's implied question. Use punchwords and specificity. Never a clean, self-contained one-liner that needs no swipe.

## 3. Do not choose an inward-looking carousel topic for a demonstration
- What went wrong: The test carousel focused on why useful carousels lose readers. The user rejected the carousel and the idea as terrible.
- Why (user's words): "I don't like this carousel at all. Like it's terrible. Use this skill for a better idea but I hate the previous one."
- How to apply: When given a broad topic, pressure-test and creatively route at least six materially different treatments before drafting. Choose a concrete audience outcome with obvious personal value, a usable mechanism, and examples. Avoid making the craft of carousel construction itself the subject unless the user asks for it.

## 5. Covers: plain and direct beats clever, every time, 2026-09-16
- Why, in the user's words: "I don't understand after reading what you've on the cover what you're talking about... it's easy to understand and accomplishes everything I want to. Why can't you understand easy and direct beats clever and clumsy."
- How to apply: Default the cover to a plain benefit/outcome statement a stranger parses in half a second — number + what it is + the result. Do NOT open with an oblique object, artefact or ritual unless the direct version has already been written and judged weaker. Curiosity is generated by the BODY paying off the promise, not by withholding what the post is about. Withholding the subject is confusion, not tension. Save the clever line for the thesis slide, where it has earned context.
- Cross-check: this does NOT override the ban on the "you're not X, you're Y" reversal, or the demand for a highlightable word. It does override any instinct toward oblique cover framing.
