# Mascots

A brand kit for carousels where a cast of five dressed mascots acts out the idea of the post. Made on 10th October 2026 from one inspiration cover and three rounds of the owner's review. **Read `Feedback.md` first: every line there is a standing rule and it wins over anything here.**

## The three laws

1. **Mascots need a purpose.** Each one goes with the concept of the post, adds to what that slide says and belongs in the scene. A mascot that adds nothing gets smaller or gets cut.
2. **Let the text breathe.** Negative space is what makes the slide feel premium. Never fill the slide from the first pixel to the last.
3. **Text first on body slides.** Mascots never take half the slide. If the body text is hard to read, the mascots or the heading are too big.

## The cast

Five characters, each with clothes and a different personality. This is fixed for covers.

| Mascot | Look | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Claude | Chunky terracotta orange blocky creature, rectangular head, two black rectangle eyes, cobalt blue track jacket | The AI being talked about |
| ChatGPT | Soft white character whose head is the interlocking knot flower, black zip jacket | Another AI, generic output, or the one voicing the objection |
| The handler | Orange mascot, butter yellow hard hat, orange safety vest, clipboard | The skill or system that runs things |
| The writer | Orange mascot, berry red beanie, round glasses, purple sweater, pencil and notebook | Anything about words |
| The designer | Orange mascot, green beret, cream turtleneck, paint brush | Anything about looks |

- **The cast changes with the topic.** Keep five, but recast them for each carousel: different jobs, hats and props, or objects and animal-like characters turned into mascots, chosen from the text and the idea being presented.
- **Extra small mascots** can fade into the background on a cover or CTA for a fun poster feel, as long as the main five stay clear.
- **Look:** soft 3D clay with rich dimensional shading, half-lidded sceptical eyes, no outlines, soft natural shadows.
- **No legs, ever.** Mascots rise from the bottom edge or from behind an object, cropped at the chest or waist.

## Type

| Use | Font | Size on a 1080 by 1440 slide |
|---|---|---|
| Cover headline | Heavy high-contrast display serif, tight leading, centred | About 95 px |
| Body slide heading | Same serif | 60 to 72 px. Big, never huge |
| Body text and bullets | Plain neutral sans in the Arial or Helvetica family, regular weight, bold for the one key phrase | 34 to 38 px, never under 32 |
| Labels and captions | Same sans | 26 to 30 px |

- The first body font (a rounded geometric sans) was rejected. The plain neutral sans is my pick from his earlier "as simple as Arial" note; he has not seen it yet.
- If the heading stops the eye from reading the rest, it is too big.
- Covers never use a two-line headline: one line, or three or more.

## Spacing

- **The border.** An imaginary border runs round all four sides and no text crosses it: top, left, right, and at the bottom the line where the mascots begin. He asked for about 20 px. The approved notebook slide sits nearer 60 px on a 1080 canvas, so treat 20 as the hard minimum and the notebook slide as the target (my reading). Keep it identical on every slide of a deck.
- **Heading to body:** a clear gap, about one line of body text.
- **Between paragraphs or bullets:** about half a line. Never stacked tight.
- **Text to mascots:** a clear empty band. On covers the arrowed labels live in that band.
- At least a third of every body slide stays empty background.

## Text on the slide

- Never change the copy. Change how it is presented.
- Long text is broken into bullets or short chunks of two lines at most. One short paragraph on a slide is the limit.
- Numbered sections are written plainly: `1. The Swipe File`. No giant outlined numerals.
- A set of numbers or stats sits in one row with even spacing, each number modest in size with its caption under it.
- Bold only the phrase a skimmer must keep.

## Mascots on the slide

| Slide | Mascot share | How |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Up to half | Five in two rows, two biggest in front, three behind, cropped by the bottom edge |
| Body | A quarter at most | One to three small mascots doing something that shows the slide's point, tucked into a corner or peeking round an object |
| CTA | A third at most | The cast adds to the scene around the ask; the ask stays the biggest thing |

Before placing a mascot, answer: what is it doing, and what does that tell the reader that the text does not? No answer means no mascot.

## Layouts

**Cover.** The handle `@yourhandle` small at the very top, centred, in the body sans. Then the headline in the top quarter, subtitle under it, a modest gap, then the five mascots. Each mascot that stands for a named thing gets a short hand-drawn curved arrow and a 1 to 3 word label in the band. Leave only a little clear space between the subtitle and the labels: `Sample cover.png` plus a tiny bit more. A big empty band was rejected.

**Format.** Every slide is 3:4 portrait, 1080 by 1440. Body slide standard: the second-round slides in `Carousels/Carousel 12`.

**Body layouts, approved concepts:**

1. **The paper object** (the best). The text lives on a cream paper object that fits the idea: a notebook page, an index card, a checklist sheet, a receipt. Slight tilt, a clip or pin, labelled rows, one red pen mark. Mascots peek over the edges.
2. **The bubble.** A cream speech or thought bubble holds the objection or quote at heading size, its tail pointing to the small mascot saying it. The answer sits beside or below in body text.
3. **The pull quote.** One quoted phrase at heading size with the rest in body text below, and a small mascot scene reacting to it.

**Body layouts still to prove:** a plain numbered heading with three or four bullets and one small mascot in the corner, and a one-row stat strip. Use them when no paper object or bubble fits.

**CTA.** Follows the same rules as a body slide: border, modest heading, readable offer text. The keyword sits in a cream comment bubble at heading size. The cast gathers small at the bottom around the thing being given.

## Never

- Text touching or near the edges, or filling every pixel
- Headings so big the body text shrinks
- Mascots over a quarter of a body slide, or mascots with nothing to do
- Unbroken blocks of text
- Giant outlined numerals
- A changed background colour
- Legs or feet, hard geometric shadows, outlines, glows, glass cards, badges

## Prompt notes for the image model

- Attach `Sample cover.png` and `References/Approved Body Slide the Notebook Page.png` to every run so the cast and the spacing match.
- State the border, the text sizes and the mascot share in words; the model fills the canvas unless told otherwise.
- Give the exact text and say "this text and nothing else".
