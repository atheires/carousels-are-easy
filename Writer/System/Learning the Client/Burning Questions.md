# The Dream Ladder and the Burning Questions

*Harvest only. Nothing is scored or filtered here.*

---

**Do not score anything in this step. Do not filter anything in this step.** This step harvests. Judgement comes later and it comes to a full page, not an empty one.

#### 2a. The Dream Ladder

**One big dream outcome, in one sentence, with a number on it wherever a number exists.**

Not a feeling. The actual thing they'd say if you asked what they're doing all this for. *"Make $10k a month from my skill, from anywhere."* The number matters — it's what makes the dream checkable to the reader, and vagueness here produces vague topics all the way down.

**Then the sub-dreams.** The things they believe they need in order to get the big one. Six to twelve of them. These are not stages of a funnel — they're the components the reader themselves thinks the outcome is made of.

For a freelance audience that's things like: a steady stream of clients · clients who are actually good · a pipeline that generates leads without them chasing · one proven offer they can pitch and close · a way to keep upskilling as AI moves · pricing they can say out loud · repeat clients instead of one-offs.

**Each sub-dream is a catalyst, and that's the whole point of writing them down.** You do not generate questions from the big dream — it's too abstract and produces four generic questions. You generate them from each sub-dream, one at a time, and each one opens its own set.

#### 2b. The Burning Questions — the burning questions

**Go sub-dream by sub-dream and write out every question this person is actually asking.** Their words, not the polished version. A burning question is one they want answered *right now* because they believe the answer moves them toward the outcome today.

Sweep each sub-dream across **all four awareness levels** — this is what stops the bank from being forty versions of the same intermediate question:

| Level | Where they are | The shape of their questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know the problem exists, or that this route exists | *"Can you actually make money doing this?" "Is it too late?" "Do I need a degree?"* Broadest reach, least intent. |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the pain, can't name the cause | *"Why is nobody replying to me?" "Why can't I get clients?" "Why am I always broke doing this?"* The richest layer — most carousels live here. |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows what kind of fix exists, doesn't know how to run it | *"How do I do outreach that works?" "How do I find businesses that can pay?" "How do I package an offer?"* |
| **Most aware** | Doing it, wants the specific optimisation | *"How do I go from $2k to $5k?" "How do I stop competing on price?" "How do I get a client to renew?"* Narrowest reach, highest intent. |

**Three multipliers to run over every sub-dream** — they turn one question into many:

- **The number ladder.** First $1 · first $100 · first client · first $1k month · $3k · $10k. Same sub-dream, six different burning questions, because the person at $0 and the person at $3k are not asking the same thing.
- **Specific vs broad.** *"What's the future of freelancing with AI"* is the broad version. *"What's the future of copywriting with AI," "…video editing," "…design," "…web development"* are four more posts, each one sharper to the person who does that skill and still readable by everyone else.
- **The how-to floor.** Include the questions that feel too simple to write about. They feel obvious to the client and they are not obvious to the reader — and they're where the volume of searching actually is.
