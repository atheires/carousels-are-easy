# Successful Post DNA

*Run whenever a client supplies a carousel that worked, or one they wish they had written. Not a step in the flow, a thing you do the moment material arrives.*

---

## What this is for

Reading a finished post to understand **how this specific writer thinks**, so future carousels come out sounding like them instead of like a system.

## The one hard rule

> **Never turn a breakdown into a template.**

The output is understanding, not a shape to fill. The moment a breakdown becomes "the six-slide structure they use," it has destroyed the thing it was meant to protect. Two posts by the same writer can share zero structure and still share a voice, and the voice is what you are here for.

If a breakdown produces something that could be applied mechanically to any topic, it was done wrong. Delete it and start again.

---

## Where the material lives

```
Clients/[name]/Successful Posts/
  [post name].md          the raw slide copy, exactly as supplied
  DNA.md                  the running breakdown across every post supplied
```

`DNA.md` is cumulative. Each new post adds to it and sharpens what is already there. Patterns that show up in one post are observations. Patterns that show up in three are DNA.

---

## What to read for

Work through all of it. Each layer answers a different question about the writer.

### 1. The shape of the argument
- What does slide 1 promise, and where exactly does the post pay it off?
- Is this a starter or a promise, and does slide 2 behave accordingly?
- What is the single idea, and how many slides pass before it appears?
- Where does the reader's belief actually flip?

### 2. Slide-to-slide movement
- **What question does each slide leave open, and does the next one answer it?**
- Where does the post accelerate, and where does it deliberately slow down?
- Is there a slide that exists only to create a pause?
- What happens at the exact midpoint?

### 3. Curiosity mechanics
- What is revealed, what is withheld, and in what order?
- Which sentence is the one that makes swiping feel involuntary?
- Does curiosity come from a gap, a contradiction, a number, or a story that has not finished?
- How long is the reader made to wait for the payoff?

### 4. Wording
- Sentence length, and how much it varies inside one slide
- Contractions, slang, profanity, filler words, the level of casualness
- Words they reach for repeatedly, and words they visibly avoid
- Punctuation habits: full stops, commas, ellipses, brackets, caps
- Do they write in fragments or in flowing sentences?

### 5. Length and density
- Words per slide, and how much that swings across the post
- Where the longest slide sits and why it earned the room
- Whether there is a single-line slide, and what job it does

### 6. Flow and continuity
- What keeps the conversation from breaking between slides?
- Are there hooks at the end of slides, or does the next one just continue?
- Repeated phrases, callbacks, a running thread
- Read it aloud: where does it stumble, and where does it not?

### 7. The reader relationship
- Are they talking to the reader, at them, or beside them?
- Where do they validate, where do they challenge, and in what ratio?
- How is authority established, and how early?
- Is there humour, and is it aimed at the reader, the advice, or themselves?

### 8. Choices, and why
This is the part that matters most and the part most likely to be skipped.

For the strongest three or four moments in the post, answer: **why this and not something else?**

- Why this line after that line?
- Why this slide after that slide?
- Why was this detail included when it slowed the post down?
- Why was this obvious thing left out?
- What would have happened if these two slides were swapped?

---

## The output

Append to `DNA.md`:

```
## [post name] — added [date]

**What it did:** one line
**The shape:** starter or promise, and how the argument moved
**Strongest moment:** the line, and why it works
**Wording signature:** the specific verbal habits visible here
**Choices worth keeping:** three or four, each with the reason
**What surprised me:** anything that contradicts the earlier breakdowns
```

Then update the top of `DNA.md` with a short **standing profile** — the traits that have now shown up more than once. That profile is what gets read before writing, not the individual breakdowns.

---

## When to read it

- Before writing any carousel for this client
- Before writing slide 1 headlines, alongside `Feedback/Slide 1.md`
- When a draft comes back feeling generic, which usually means the profile was not read
