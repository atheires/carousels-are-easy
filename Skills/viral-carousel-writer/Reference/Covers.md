# Covers

The cover decides whether the rest is read. It has three jobs: say what this is, say why a stranger should care now, and make it feel quick to read. Every rule here exists because a cover was rejected.

## 1. What the winners' covers measure

From `Numbers.md` (116 posts; all figures pattern-detected, +/-5 points):

| Measure | First 71 | Later posts | All |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median words on the cover | 11 | 15 | 12.5 |
| Covers of 10 words or fewer | 47% | 30% | 40% |
| Covers of 11 to 20 words | 38% | 48% | 42% |
| Covers of 21 to 40 words | 15% | 23% | 18% |
| Main headline, average words (first sentence or phrase) | 11.1 | 11.6 | 11.3 |
| Main headline, median words | 9.5 | 10.5 | 10 |
| Has a subheadline (anything after the first sentence) | 26% | 39% | 31% |
| Subheadline, average words when present | 7.9 | 10.4 | 9.1 |
| Contains a digit | 29% | 57% | 40% |
| Carries a limit or credential ($64/month, 1000 hours, <3 min) | 9% | 20% | 14% |
| Contains a parenthesis (a kicker) | 5% | 14% | 8% |

**How to read it.** A cover is one short sentence of about 10 words, sometimes with a short line under it of about 9 words that adds the stake, the proof or the limit. Over 20 words is a minority and almost always a story or a stacked promise. The newer posts add proof (a digit, a credential) and a parenthetical kicker such as `(in <3 minutes)`, `(+FREE Templates at the end)`, `(here's everything I learned)`, `(and how you can too)`.

**Cover angle, as used (primary angle; a cover can sit in several):**

| Angle | Share, all posts |
|---|---|
| How to | 36% |
| Plain claim or topic statement | 15% |
| Number-led list ("9 things…") | 10% |
| Confession or personal story ("I…", "my…") | 8% |
| Reader callout ("you…") | 8% |
| Stop / Never / Don't | 5% (0% in the later posts) |
| Mistake or what's wrong | 5% |
| Named concept or levels | 4% |
| How I | 3% (4% in the later posts) |
| Enemy or attack | 3% |
| Question | 2% |
| X vs Y | 1% |
| Why | under 1% |

How to is the workhorse, and it is not the boring option: a plain "How to [outcome the reader wants]" is a legitimate, frequently winning cover. Stop, Never, Why and Question covers nearly vanished from the later posts. The number carries more of the cover than the angle does.

## 2. Cover shapes by post type

| If the post is… | Cover shape winners used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A process | `How to [result] ([tight limit])` | "How to use LinkedIn Search to get hired (in <3 minutes)" (104) |
| A personal process with a proof number | `How I [result] ([proof])` | "how i create 6 carousels in under 1 hour" (95); "I SPENT 1000 HOURS VIBE CODING (here's everything I learned)" (81) |
| A list | `[Number] [things] [with stakes or a surprising object]` | "9 Brutal Career Truths You Learn a Little Late in Life" (103) |
| A cost-bounded build | `The exact [thing] I'd use if I only had [limit]` | "…content engine if I only had $64/month" (106) |
| A belief shift | A claim that stings, with the reader named or implied | "Perfect looks fake now" (72); "5 of these AI agents are a waste of money" (61) |
| A named framework | `[N] levels of [thing]` with the top level promised | "3 Levels of Branding" (73); "6 levels of storytelling… the higher you climb, the more addictive" (109) |
| A story | A before and an after, with a number as the hinge | "I set an aggressive goal to hit 10K followers in 1 week & hit it in 1 day" (75) |
| A permission post | Names the reader's blocker so they can start | "NO CLIENTS? NO EXPERIENCE? NO PROBLEM" (44) |
| A large task that is the burden | Just the task | "HOW TO PLAN A FULL MONTH OF CONTENT" (46) |

## 3. Cover openers that worked, by the tension they use

| Opener | Example |
|---|---|
| Negative-frame list | "Hacks I'd gatekeep if I wanted ADHD entrepreneurs to fail" (20) |
| Diagnostic: make them check their own data | "Check your last 10 carousels right now." (19) |
| Attack what the audience loves | "CLAUDE SUCKS AT DESIGN." (08) |
| Prohibition | "Never let a client's urgency become your emergency." (32) |
| Attack a habit they have by name | "Stop asking your clients 'What's your budget?'" (33) |
| Paradox that accuses a virtue | "Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable" (22) |
| Question from the buyer's side | "Why on earth would I wire you $18k?" (12) |
| Strange concrete fact, withheld reason | "I watched a woman buy TWO birthday cakes for herself." (11) |
| Dated personal observation | "This morning I opened my IG explore page and I'm in shock." (13) |
| Confession that is actually an offer | "I can't keep this to myself anymore." (35) |
| Subtraction | "It's not an app. It's not a plugin. It's just 3 folders." (09; this is a negation then a correction, so it is on the banned list. It is evidence of what a winner did, not permission to do it) |
| Count, audience and outcome | "I BUILT 9 CLAUDE SKILLS THAT EVERY ONLINE MARKETER NEEDS" (03) |
| Unserious adviser beside a heavy topic | "how to deal with a bad mental health day / advice from a goose" (51) |
| A threatened standard | "The era of good marketing is over." (52) |
| Borrowed ease for an unwanted outcome | "how to waste your marketing budget / in 6 easy steps" (55) |
| The reader's own bill | "how an Instagram page can pay your rent IN SEPTEMBER" (116) |

The best filter found (16): *People don't swipe for information. They swipe to solve a problem or satisfy curiosity.* "Here are some tips" is information. "Stop using Pinterest. Here's why." is a promise.

**Generative method for contrarian hooks (19):** identify the most common advice in the niche, then write five hooks that argue the opposite. It complements the why-chain: the chain finds the fear, this finds the received wisdom.

## 4. The cover rules

1. **Plain beats clever.** If a stranger cannot say what the post is about in two seconds without decoding an image, a number or a turn of phrase, it is wrong, however well it scores on curiosity. Hide the method, never the subject. (Feedback F002; `cover-plain-beats-clever`.)
2. **Default to the direct version first.** Number or limit, what it is, the result. Only consider an oblique, artefact-led or ritual-led cover after the direct version has been written and judged weaker. Save the clever line for the thesis slide.
3. **Never "you're not X, you're Y"**, in any form, on any slide (F001, F003).
4. **The parenthetical kicker is a proven device.** It kills the effort objection before the swipe: `(+FREE Templates at the end)`, `(in 8 weeks)`, `(takes 90 seconds at the end of any chat)`, `(in <3 minutes)`.
5. **A hedge, not a superlative.** "(almost) Anything", "hardly anyone". A flat promise reads as a marketer.
6. **The number is a credential or a limit,** and the body must honour it (R6).
7. **Aim at the biggest group's belief, fear, wish or doubt,** never the perfect belief only a handful hold. Lean negative frames for reach (social rewards them), keep some positive covers for variety.
8. **Know the viewer from the inside:** what they believe, what they ask, what worries and what they doubt, what they still do that they no longer need to, what they fear and strive for. Then do one of four things: hit them where it hurts most, paint a dream they badly need, promise something they want, or remove a doubt they hold.
9. **A concrete provocation owes its explanation on the next slide** (R21).
10. **Word choice.** Short, five to ten words for the main line; past twelve something is hiding in a clause. A subheadline is optional and depends on the rest of the carousel; add one only if the carousel needs it (to add proof, stakes or a limit).
11. **The cover's idea leads, its wording is finalised last.** Slide 1's idea decides the carousel; the final wording waits until the body is written.
12. **Cover must match the argument visually.** A cover image or look unrelated to the argument is a fault (14). A post that claims taste must demonstrate taste (see `Craft Moves.md`, visual register).

## 5. Angle groups (what each needs to work)

**Group 1, the safe six.** Almost any idea fits and they are hard to get wrong. Default here.

| Angle | What it is |
|---|---|
| How to | The method, promised plainly |
| How I | A real result plus how it was done |
| List | A counted set |
| Mistake | The one error that ruins the result. Reach for it first when the idea has a common way of going wrong |
| Checklist | What to do or say before starting |
| Worst case | The fear, then the fix |

**Group 2, the thought experiment.** Sits just below the safe six. Works when it captures the exact thing the viewer wants to be able to do, in few words: "Imagine describing an animation and getting exactly what you wanted". Too long and it dies.

**Group 3, hard to pull off and unbeatable when right.** Each works only if you have the viewer's mind exactly right.

| Angle | It works only when |
|---|---|
| Named concept | The concept is one the viewer already thinks about and wants to learn |
| Command | You found the exact perspective shift they need, and tell them not to do the old thing |
| Bold statement | It gets a "whaaat", which needs their past, present, future and wants |
| Reader callout | You know what the biggest group is still doing that they don't need to |
| Question | They feel connected to it, framed positively or negatively |
| Result promise | It is a result the whole audience wants, not a side benefit |
| Myth | They genuinely believe the thing |
| Unpopular opinion | You flip something they think is good to bad (or the reverse). Never write the words "unpopular opinion" on the cover; state the opinion, then add "let me explain" |
| Borrowed authority | The authority is a real, known name; their goodwill and badwill both come with them. An anonymous authority carries no weight |
| Enemy | The perfect enemy, shared by most viewers. The most powerful angle when it lands |
| Common enemy quote | The perfect enemy, quoted |
| Reverse advice | The outcome named is something they genuinely hate; easy to get badly wrong |
| Confession | A confession the viewer also thinks, followed by a reframe that makes them reject it |
| Trend | A trend they are already noticing; finding it is the craft |
| One-word reframe | The viewer already knows the original word and idea; very hard |
| Objection first | Their exact, biggest objection |
| Audience label | The label is exactly right |
| Things I stopped doing | The things named are what they are doing right now and still not getting the result |

**Group 4, conditional.** Rarely used; only when the idea is screaming for it: Why, Story, Test, If/then, Before and after, X vs Y, What nobody tells you, Letter to past self, Prediction, Cost or warning, Analogy, Permission, Timeframe, Definition, Signs you're doing it wrong, Number or data, Case study, Dialogue, Paradox, Rule, Things I'd tell you, Small moment, Screenshot or receipt, Two people, Countdown, Metric.

## 6. Corrections on record (real misses on a motion-graphics idea)

| Angle | The miss | Why it missed |
|---|---|---|
| Reader callout | "You're still opening After Effects for this?" | Aimed at a small group; most viewers settle for low-quality output |
| Result promise | "Your first motion graphic, finished in the next 15 minutes" | Promised speed; what they want is quality with less effort |
| Borrowed authority | "A motion designer watched Claude build this and went quiet." | Anonymous authority carries no weight |
| Unpopular opinion | "Unpopular opinion: you no longer need to learn After Effects." | The label on the cover looks cheap |
| Reverse advice | "How to keep spending 6 hours on a 10-second animation" | The outcome is not something they genuinely hate |
| Thought experiment | "Imagine describing an animation out loud and getting it back finished." | Too long |

Plus a health-account miss: oblique covers "my hair routine starts the night before, in two jars of water" and "the paper on my fridge with five times on it is my whole hair routine" were rejected. The cover he supplied: **"6 Things I Eat for Faster Hair Growth."** Number, what it is, the result.

## 7. How to write the covers for a job

1. Write down the viewer: who sees this, and what the largest group believes, fears, wants and doubts.
2. Search the library for the angle: `python "Scripts/Search Library.py" --angle "How to" "<topic words>"`, then read three covers and their notes.
3. **Default delivery:** the one best cover chosen from the real options, plus two alternates in different angles, each with a one-line reason naming the bet it makes on the viewer. When the user asks for covers, give the six-row table: two safe covers first, then four from Group 3 (or one from Group 4 if the idea is screaming for it), each hard angle with its named bet; if a bet is a guess, say so.
4. For each, the viewer must know what the post is about from the cover alone. Plain beats clever. Keep it short.
5. "Change it" or "another" means a different angle of the same type; never reword the same angle or bring back one turned down for this idea.
6. A cover edit by the user is a lesson: record the pattern in `Feedback Log.md` the same turn, without discussing it.
