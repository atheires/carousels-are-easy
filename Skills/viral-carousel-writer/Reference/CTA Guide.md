# CTA Guide

How the ask works: what kinds exist, what each hands over, how the best are framed and what fails. Numbers are from `Numbers.md`; the measured comment rates are Tinna Loaiza's.

## 1. What winners do (116 posts, CTA read from the last three slides)

| CTA | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Comment a word | 60% (58% of the first 71, 64% of the later set) | The workhorse. 97% of keywords are a single word, 72% are in capitals, average 5.9 letters (5.2 to 6.9) |
| No CTA, ends on the idea | 19% | A deliberate close (36, 47, 51, 56, 112), not an omission |
| Follow only | 9% (12% to 5%) | Falling |
| Apply, email, book or link | 7% | A post that is itself an offer (76, 99) |
| Save or share only | 3% | Works when it names a future use (58, 92, 101, 111) |
| DM a word | 2% | When delivery needs a conversation (88, 93, 97) |

93% of keyword CTAs sit on the last slide; about 26% also ask earlier as a second ask or a coda.

**What a comment or DM CTA hands over** (a CTA can name several; first 71, later set, all):

| Offer | First 71 | Later | All |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guide, playbook, doc or framework | 41% | 41% | 41% |
| Video, course or workshop | 10% | 31% | 19% |
| Template, sheet, list or link | 23% | 21% | 22% |
| Paid offer, cohort, waitlist or spot | 10% | 24% | 16% |
| Prompts | 10% | 7% | 9% |
| Tool, dashboard or skill | 10% | 3% | 7% |

**How it is framed** (among comment or DM CTAs): the deliverable and its form are named in 13% overall, 21% of the later set; free is stated in 16% (24% of the later set); a scarcity or deadline appears in 4% (10% of the later set); the benefit in the reader's life is spelled out in 4% (7% later). These are detected on phrases, so they undercount; the strongest CTAs in the library nearly all do at least two of these.

The direction of travel: from "comment for the prompts" towards "comment for the video, the playbook or a place in the offer", with a named deliverable and, when it is real, a number or a date.

## 2. Which kind to use

| CTA | Use when | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| Comment a word, get the thing | The post taught the idea and a bigger usable version exists (a video, a prompt pack, a guide, a template) | 80, 81, 91, 95, 105, 113 |
| Comment a word, get a place in the offer | The post is the story of the offer (waitlist, cohort, application, workshop) | 73, 89, 99, 102, 106 |
| DM a word | Delivery needs a conversation or a longer asset | 88, 93, 97 |
| Reply to the post's own question | You want comments, not conversions | 72, 79, 83 |
| Follow or save, with a reason | The post is a reference; the reason names a future use | 84, 92, 111 |
| No CTA | The close is the point and an ask would weaken it | 36, 47, 51, 56, 112 |
| Apply or send | The post is itself an offer | 76, 99 |
| Reread and report | The post performed its own lesson (79) | 79 |

The choice between the first two is decided by the sentence after the keyword: either "I'll send you X" or "I'll send you the details to see if it's a good fit".

## 3. How the best ones are written

1. **The CTA answers "what does this do for me?".** The carousel has just created a specific need. Name that need as the reader's, say the thing removes it (in their life, tonight), then ask in one plain word. Cut the corporate tells: "starter version", "template", "resource", "framework" as catalogue nouns, "It's yours", "I'll send it over". Say what it does instead. "Fill it in once and you never explain your business to Claude again" beats any description of the file. (F004)
2. **Name the deliverable and its form.** "my brand new video lesson revealing how to build a personal brand from scratch" (113); "22 MIN FULL GUIDE" (81); "2000+ handpicked viral hooks" (87); "FREE income audit template" (86); "Schema file. Folder structure. The exact prompt that builds it." (09).
3. **Make the keyword the reader's own conclusion.** BASE (113, the thing to build from), LADDER (109, the framework just taught), LEVEL (73), HABITS (82), REDDIT (94), DASHBOARD (90); WISDOM, ARC, STORYLINE (Tinna). Typing the word is agreement. Seed it one slide earlier ("You're starting from wisdom" then WISDOM). A word nobody could connect to the post (66's MELON) worked only because the offer answered the thesis.
4. **Seed the gap in the last two slides, not the first.** Teach everything the reader can use tonight, then name the single bigger piece you hold back. Manufacture the gap fresh at the end ("Every year you've been alive is storytelling gold… you just need to know how to mine it" took Tinna's Post 4 to 1,903 comments, 12.9% of likes).
5. **Hide only the assembly.** The parts are on the slides; the sequencing, the full prompt, the real examples, the template and the judgment about which to pick sit behind the word. Tinna: "She rarely hides the idea. She hides the judgment required to execute it well." Also viable: completeness gating (04: the cover diagram labels eight parts, the post teaches three, the CTA sells the missing five) and level gating (29: deliver a complete method then reveal it is the beginner version).
6. **Giving the how away does not kill the CTA.** 06, 07 and 09 print full prompts and files and still convert. What is sold is the copy-paste, not the knowledge. Specificity is the proof (a prompt naming `decisions.md`, `people.md`, `preferences.md` cannot be bluffed).
7. **Show the withheld thing without giving it.** A grid of blank folders (03), a rendered comment box (08), a stack of PDFs (25).
8. **Add a number or a deadline when the offer is real.** "send by September 6th" (76), "3 spots open for September" (89), "only 50 people. Once they're gone, they're gone" (99), "$850 / normally $5,000" (99), "JUNE 28TH 10:00A MST" (106). **Never invent scarcity.**
9. **Escalate at the last moment.** "(Plus 50+ others)" (03), "+ Get 3 bonus PDF Guides" (25), "+5 more suggestions!" (57).
10. **Give a friction fix.** "(follow me first or it lands in your requests)" is a real favour; the references use it (116: "make sure to follow me so my message comes through").
11. **Widen right before the ask.** Give four audiences a line each so nobody excludes themselves (09, 24).
12. **Gratitude immediately before the ask** worked in 12 and 35.
13. **Place it on the last slide.** An earlier CTA works only as a coda or a second ask (60, 62, 107); front-loading on slide 1 is viable (28), buying coverage from bouncers at some cost to curiosity.
14. **One word per offer.** Repeat the same word across posts for the same asset (STORY 24 and 25, LOVED 22 and 27, BOOK 31, 32, 33). Never across assets.

## 4. Strongest and weakest by design

Strongest (both reads agree on the design, not on measured conversion): 89 APPLY after "3 spots open"; 102 JOIN or WAITLIST after the fake-work-becomes-real-work payoff and a specified challenge; 80 Infographic after a free tutorial preview; 107 HOOK after the checklist-versus-full-build distinction; 90 dashboard after a personalised build recipe. Measured strong in the earlier sample: 08 DESIGN (15,936 comments on 9,542 likes), 07 CLAUDE (14,913 on 8,321), 05 ADS, 03 SKILLS, each a named countable artefact plus a short keyword from the post's own vocabulary.

Weakest: a bare "CLAUDE" with no stated reward (91); a "2000+ hooks" offer with a weak bridge from a three-slide frame (87); a "BRAND" playbook that does not match the slides' topic (97); "Comment 🔥 if you want more" (14, 15, 17), which names nothing; "field notes of what I'm building" (30), which describes the author's activity and hands the reader nothing.

## 6. What fails

- "Comment 🔥 if you want more" names nothing.
- A CTA that describes the author's activity gives the reader nothing.
- A CTA that arrives before the reader has the proof or the lesson.
- A keyword nobody could connect to the post.
- A principle taught and not followed (14 to 17).
- Two competing asks that dilute each other (60's COPYMATE, then DM COPY, then a follow, works only because a coda is clearly a coda).
- An ask that spends attention on the account when the post argued for less of it (36, 47, 51, 56 end without one).

## 7. Writing the CTA for a job

1. Is this a reach post or a lead post (R8)? If reach, choose no CTA or a save or follow with a named future use.
2. If lead, what real asset or offer exists? If none has been given, do not invent one: write an assumed deliverable in the draft, mark it ADD, and tell the user in "Needs you" what you assumed.
3. Search the library for the same kind of offer: `python "Scripts/Search Library.py" --cta "Comment a word" --offer "video" "cta"`; read two CTA slides and the slide before them.
4. Draft the last two slides: seed the gap, then the ask. Name the deliverable and its form, give the benefit in their life, one plain keyword that is their own conclusion.
5. Check the keyword was seeded one slide earlier, the ask is one ask, and nothing described is invented.
