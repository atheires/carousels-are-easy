# Craft Moves

How the winners do each job, indexed by the job you are doing. Every move cites the posts it came from; read the post with `python "Scripts/Search Library.py" --slides NN` before borrowing the move. A move is a decision made for one idea, never a template: take the reason, not the wording.

The long-form read behind this file is `Carousel DNA.md` (all 113 first-read posts, 57KB), and Tinna Loaiza's cross-post synthesis is in `Library/Tinna Loaiza/00 - DNA Synthesis.md`.

## 1. Opening and the first two slides

- **Spend the cover on the subject and a stake.** See `Covers.md` for shapes. A large task can be hook enough ("HOW TO PLAN A FULL MONTH OF CONTENT", 46). A cover can assume a lost start and refuse a huge effort ("and pull that comeback" with "Start small", 57).
- **Name the circular prerequisite that stops the reader starting** ("NO CLIENTS? NO EXPERIENCE? NO PROBLEM", 44) or admit the reader whose history disqualifies them ("EVEN IF YOU'VE FALLEN OFF BEFORE", 66).
- **Make the endangered asset the thing the post protects** ("HOW TO STAY HUMAN IN THE AGE OF AI" then "READ SOMETHING LONG", 36).
- **Withhold the profession, expose the detour** ("at 30 / i am finally becoming what I wanted to be at 7", 53).
- **A big number and a small admission** ("hit it in 1 day using Claude" plus "I am no Claude expert, but here is exactly what I did", 75): the reader expects a hype post and gets a walkthrough.
- **Pre-empt the objection on slide 1 only when the doubt would end the read** (10). Otherwise save objections for the chain (04).
- **Slide 2 is where trust is bought.** Proof, the old belief, the frame, or the fix said first. See `Formats and Slide Shapes.md` section 4.
- **A front-loaded CTA on slide 1 is viable** (28) but costs a little curiosity.

## 2. Flow and transitions

- **The question chain, written out.** Post 09 ends every slide with a forward line ("But folders alone won't work without this →", "Now you need to feed it →"), seven links unbroken. Post 04 uses dangling objections: "But it doesn't have any type of personality…" answered, then "But so far this isn't any different from a system prompt…" answered. The last line states the reader's next doubt in their words; the next slide resolves it. The final link before a CTA can be an instruction: "Save this before you forget it →".
- **A label chain tells time without a numbered list.** 75's headings run "I started by giving it everything" then "Then we argued about my pillars" then "But every pillar had to tie back" then "Then I told it exactly what I wanted": each starts with Then or But.
- **A dependency can replace an explicit link.** 44's repeated "You don't need" removes a different dependency each time (paid collaboration, a supplied product, a 50-page portfolio). 46 answers capacity, days, mix, formats and openings by filling the same week. 68 needs no dangling question because noticing an invitation precedes accepting it.
- **A pivot slide with four words on it.** "Here's the better move:" (32, 33).
- **Split a contrast across two slides.** The negative and the positive get a swipe between them (33).
- **The same unfinished object gets a decision added each time.** 46's "M Actionable tip" becomes "M Actionable tip | Carousel", then receives an opening: the reader sees a calendar become specific.
- **A concession locates the target before the attack.** "By the way: I LOVE AI." (12); "That sounds normal…" (33); "I have SO much respect for you" (34); "And I'm not saying technical skills don't matter. (They do)." (60). The qualification must carry reasoning, not just politeness.
- **Put review where execution could exceed intention** (59 "REVIEW, THEN MOVE TO DISTRIBUTION", 69 "Run it once before making it automatic").
- **Questions within an item are not a chain across the post.** 51's separate situations are local; do not force a chain onto a list.

## 3. Shifting a belief

The sequence the winners use: the reader's old belief in their words, why it is reasonable, the break, the replacement.

- **Quote the enemy line the reader has heard.** 13: "Reels are dead, yapping is in"; 32: "We'll make it worth your while."; 113: "What should I talk about?". Recognition does the arguing.
- **Swap the reader's question.** "Most people stop at 'does this look realistic?' Wrong question." (18). "The market isn't asking 'Why are you so expensive?' They're asking 'Why are you worth it?'" (31).
- **Intercept the misreading in the same breath.** "The customer is not unimpressed. They are unaware." (22). "That is not inconsistency." (27). (These are corrections of a misreading, not a banned "you're not X, you're Y" about the reader's identity; keep them only where the misreading is real and specific.)
- **Redefine the feared response as information.** "Some will pay it. Some will suddenly discover the deadline was flexible. Both answers are correct." (32).
- **Show a vocabulary change, do not instruct it.** Budget to investment, demonstrated across two slides and never explained (33).
- **Make the reader supply material before interpreting it** (37: "Just think of what you'd actually say").
- **Let the apparent happy ending be wrong twice** (53: "Ticked every box" and "living the dream" both fail before the teacher ambition resolves).
- **Let a repeated word acquire a different object.** 38's "share my earnings" becomes "share the choices behind my work"; 52 repeats "good" until an advantage becomes a baseline; 53's "aim higher" returns inside "I'd picked it up from my feed".
- **Remove the easy explanation.** 75 follows its win with "None of it worked because of just a prompt" and five human causes. Specific credit makes a win believable and a method less magical.
- **Redefine value with a second name for the problem.** 75: "I did not have a reach problem. I had a 'nobody who sees this knows why they should stay' problem." 63: "The problem isn't that your branding looks bad. It's that it looks familiar." (The second name is the idea; the sentence frame there is the banned shape, so write the idea in another form.)
- **Separate meaning from obligation.** 100: "You don't have to keep him to keep the expansion."
- **A counterfactual institution states priorities as a curriculum.** 111's "If we ran a design school".
- **Tie the money to a value, not a pitch.** 92 ties childhood moments to "the time to be there", so the money offer arrives as a value.
- **Validate before you pivot.** Agree with the decision they made and name where the belief came from (D11 in the old vault, still true).

## 4. Proving it

- **Put the receipt on slide 2 or 3** (102 `$896K+`; 75 `0.13%` to `0.83%`; 89 `$411,293`; 04 a labelled diagram).
- **Borrowed proof at scale.** Fifteen screenshots of other people's real posts and zero assertions (25). A statistic about what the audience already tried beats a results statistic (35: "60% had already created AI avatars before joining").
- **The specificity is the proof.** A prompt naming `decisions.md`, `people.md`, `preferences.md` cannot be bluffed; the proof and the product are the same object (06, 07, 09).
- **Show what the filter rejects.** A cutoff line with losers below it proves the system has judgement (28).
- **Constrain it.** "$64/month, free options first, then upgrade triggers" (106) gives a cold viewer a starting configuration, not an inventory.
- **Receipts as visible artefacts.** The screenshot of the payout (116), embedded analytics and posts (39), a before-and-after diagram (28).
- **Admit the limit.** "just look at my numbers lol" (57); 65's "you haven't lost four hours… you've lost fifteen minutes" makes the cost of failing part of the permission.

## 5. Teaching a method

- **A worked example in full, once.** 86's client email, 95's good and bad prompt, 99's maths, 69's RAOS example. An abstraction gets one complete instance before delegating execution.
- **Show the upgrade.** Raw input, what they would normally write, what it becomes; then a second example so it transfers (D14).
- **A repeated frame makes items comparable and retrievable** (84 WHAT, WHY, HOW, EXAMPLE; 81 title, tagline, three bullets; 96).
- **Cumulative levels keep the earlier ones** (109: "Keep the facts + hooks. Now add stakes.") so the reader can place themselves and see the next upgrade.
- **Teach by selection when repurposing** (108: "I pull out the lines worth stopping for").
- **Ownership by time.** 110's keep-or-offload pairs rest on order: read first, then summarise; inspect the raw numbers first, then ask for "a second pair of eyes".
- **Organise parallel actions by distance from one event** (47: "10-12hrs before bed" to "1hr before bed").
- **Reformat the plan into the reader's next scale of work** (64: "Days 4-7: create 2-3 examples", "10 targeted outreaches / day").
- **A recap that reformats rather than repeats** (09, 15, 16, 18, 73 slide 11, 102 slide 9).
- **A closing checklist** where the reader's next move is a list.

## 6. Telling a story

- **A strange concrete fact, the reason withheld** (11: "I watched a woman buy TWO birthday cakes for herself."). Eight slides of story before one of meaning.
- **Be the witness, not the hero** (11: the narrator only stands in a queue). **Let the wrong guess be yours** ("I thought maybe it was for her grandkids… But her answer surprised me.").
- **Quote people verbatim.** Post 11's three quotes beat anything the narrator says.
- **Name the feeling under the behaviour** ("underneath all of it is the same rooted feeling: Fear.", 13).
- **A story almost always leads to an offer** (75, 83, 89); build the close for it.
- **The wrong guess, the correction and the real reason** build trust faster than the success (75's failure-first order).
- **Every detail they gave you stays in or is deliberately cut.** Detail is the difference between a story and a summary.

## 7. Line-level writing

- **The paired-verdict line**, used once and only when the evidence earns it: "Liked gets ignored. Wanted gets chosen." (26); "Once is an anecdote. Every time is a belief." (22). The clauses compress a demonstrated distinction; parallelism alone is not the finding.
- **Anaphora to stack frustrations** ("Ideas die… Research dies… Design dies… Posting dies…", 28), **raise the price in the last parallel clause** (26).
- **Two tiers.** A large assertion, a small elaboration (11, 12, 30, 31). Optional.
- **Invent nothing, but name the shape of their mess** ("20 saved AI tools · 10 browser tabs open · 5 paid subscriptions · 100 prompts saved somewhere", 34, the numbers being a plausible illustration of a recognisable state, never claimed as a fact about the author).
- **The second bullet is where the life goes** (10: mechanism, then their Tuesday).
- **The surprising final word reverses the value of an ordinary act** ("MAKE SOMETHING / BADLY", 36; "go to bed early out of pure spite", 51; "beautiful. what are we selling?", 55).
- **Relief can be precise without being flattering.** "You don't need 50 pages" (44); "Be honest with yourself" (46); "today's goal is simply: do not completely lose the plot" (51).
- **Humour needs a consistent target** (51's "it has no qualifications" attacks the harsh inner voice; 55 mocks the operator's anxious overconfidence through "eleven minutes", never the customer's stupidity).
- **First-person fallibility changes who is being judged** (57 "I'm lacking here myself"; 66 "I used to be terrible at this"; 58's repeated "i" reports precautions rather than issuing orders).
- **Rhythm is a speaker's movement, not alternating lengths by count** (62 demonstrates with "One, you're not AI" and a rambling sentence ending "like what I'm doing now"). **Write like a screenwriter, not a marketer** (19).
- **Callbacks move the argument** (63 "this sheep" to "lost in the herd" to "refuses to follow the herd"; 68 "An introduction. An idea. An invitation").
- **Use a hedge to leave room** (68's "SOMETIMES" and "MAYBE"; 71's "This does not always mean you are not good enough").

## 8. Structural devices

- Open and close on the same image (07). Withheld version, then the thing (05). Before and after diagram (28). Continuous numbering across slides (08: 01 to 42) and a filling progress bar (09). Count in the headline, defended immediately (10: "Why 6, not 50 exercises"). A footer line summarising each layer (08). Widen right before the ask (09, 24). Gratitude immediately before the ask (12, 35). Non-consecutive samples that imply the rest exist (24). A relationship frame carried through marketing psychology (79). Performing the lesson on the reader and ending on "go back & read this again" (79, 108). Sequence as the argument (110). A ladder with an upgrade at each rung (109). A named framework the reader can repeat (73, 87, 97, 100, 109).

## 9. Visual register (when copy has to suit a design)

A post that claims taste must demonstrate taste (08 as a brutalist spec sheet; 12 leaves typos in; 18 uses a moody imperfect photograph; 10 is a doctor on exercise in clinical greyscale). A post that claims capability can be plain (03). Visual friction is one demonstrated strategy (19: "If the text is 'Simple,' the image should be 'Complex.'"), and a thesis can be a found artefact (34: an iPhone Notes screenshot). Choose the low-status example for the biggest claim (23: a cheap red plastic bag under the grandest line about packaging). Mark where proof visuals go with `[Screenshot: …]` in the copy. For copy only, remember that slides lecture and captions confide.
