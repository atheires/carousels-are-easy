# 13 — This Morning I Opened My IG Explore Page And I'm In Shock

**Account:** @wildmuse / Emily Huberts Consulting
**URL:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DavEpnFkVY2/
**Slides:** 8
**Type:** opinion post, same brand and format as [[12 - If Claude Writes Your Content]].

---

## Slide copy, verbatim

**1** — This morning I opened my IG explore page and I'm in *shock*. It's like the entire industry has lost its mind.

**2** — Every other hook is "Reels are dead, yapping is in" or "Claude just killed Canva" or "this is the next big thing and if you miss it your business is done"

**3** — The whole online business and coaching world is in a constant state of debate about the format, the trend, the algorithm shift nobody can actually prove is happening.

**4** — And underneath all of it is the same rooted feeling: *Fear*. What if we miss the next big thing‼ What if everyone else figures it out before we do!

**5** — So everyone stays trend focused. Chasing whatever someone screenshotted from a guru's post last week and is now selling back to their audience as the *'secret'* nobody else knows.

**6** — Meanwhile there's a much smaller group of people who aren't playing that game at all. They're not asking what's about to blow up. They're building something that was *never* designed to depend on a trend in the first place.

**7** — They're focused on what actually holds a brand together. Lived experience. Storytelling. Values you had way before you had a business. *Connection* people feel because they recognize themselves in you.

**8** — Here's what I know for sure. Trends will keep coming and cycling. The platform will keep shifting what it rewards. But *nobody* has ever built a category defining brand by chasing whatever was 'working' that week.

---

## Notes

**The hook is a timestamp and a reaction, not a claim.** *"This morning I opened my IG explore page and I'm in shock."* It reports something that happened hours ago to a thing the reader also opens daily. **A dated personal observation makes an opinion feel like reporting.**

**Slide 2 is the evidence, and it is three real quotes.** *"Reels are dead, yapping is in"*, *"Claude just killed Canva"*, *"this is the next big thing and if you miss it your business is done"*. Not a paraphrase of what the industry says — the actual sentences, in quotation marks. The reader recognises all three because they scrolled past them this week. **Quote the enemy verbatim and you never have to argue that the enemy exists.**

**Slide 4 names the emotion under the behaviour.** *"And underneath all of it is the same rooted feeling: Fear."* Everything before it is symptom; this is the diagnosis. It is also the only one-word italicised noun in the post. This is the why-chain landing: not "people chase trends" but "people are afraid of being last".

**The turn is on slide 6 and it is a group, not an instruction.** *"Meanwhile there's a much smaller group of people who aren't playing that game at all."* The reader is not told what to do — they are shown a room they could be standing in instead. **Offering membership beats offering advice.**

**Slide 7 lists what the alternative is made of, in fragments.** *"Lived experience. Storytelling. Values you had way before you had a business."* This is the one place the staccato fragment style is used, and it works because it is a list of abstractions being pinned down quickly, not a story being told. Then it resolves into a full sentence — *"Connection people feel because they recognize themselves in you."*

**It closes on a statement of certainty, not a CTA.** *"Here's what I know for sure."* After 7 slides of criticism, an authority stake. And the final clause is the sharpest thing in the post: *"nobody has ever built a category defining brand by chasing whatever was 'working' that week."*

**Eight slides, one sentence each, no headings, no bullets, no images.** The entire design is a serif on brown leather. When the writing is this quotable, the design's only job is to get out of the way — and the identical framing across all eight slides makes the post read like pages of one letter rather than eight separate points.

**Same as post 12: no CTA.** Two posts from this account, neither with an ask. The pattern for this brand is *the argument is the advertisement* — anyone who agrees hard enough will go and find the consulting page themselves.
