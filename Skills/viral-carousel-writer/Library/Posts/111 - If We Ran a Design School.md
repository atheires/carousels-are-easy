## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**

*If we ran a design school,*

this is what we'd teach

*Illustration of a fluffy pink cat with big eyes reading a book, on a blue-to-orange gradient.*

**2–6 — the roadmap (one yellow dashed line on blue grid paper joins the months)**

*Month 1*  
**Principles before tools.**  
Hierarchy, contrast, alignment, spacing. The things that don't change no matter what replaces Figma in 5 years.  
Most people start with tutorials. We'd start here.

*Month 2*  
**Typography and colour as communication.**  
Why this typeface feels safe. Why this red creates urgency. Not decoration.  
Tools. You can't make the right decision until you understand what each one is saying.

*Month 3*  
**Systems thinking.**  
Designing one screen is easy. Designing 40 connected screens where every decision affects the next one that's the actual job.  
This is where most designers plateau.

*Month 4*  
**AI fluency. The one missing from every curriculum right now.**  
Not how to use AI tools. How to design around them. Conversational UI.  
Generative interfaces. What to do when the output isn't fixed it's probabilistic.  
Every designer will touch this in 2026. Zero schools are teaching it properly yet.

*Month 5*  
**Strategy before execution.**  
What is the actual problem? Who has it? What does success look like? Figma opens last. Not first.

*Month 6*  
**Content design. Words are design decisions.**  
The error message. The empty state. The onboarding headline. Most schools give this to someone else.  
We wouldn't. The designer who controls both the layout and the language is twice as hard to replace.

*Month 7*  
**Data literacy.**  
A/B results. Heatmaps. Drop-off rates. Not to become an analyst to never be overruled in a meeting again.  
The designer who brings a heatmap to a decision conversation is unkillable.

*Month 8*  
**Presenting and defending your decisions.**  
The designer who can't explain their choices is always vulnerable.  
The one who can always wins the room.  
This gets zero time in most curriculums.

*Month 9*  
**Building in public.**  
The designers winning in 2026 aren't the ones with the best portfolios. They're the ones people already know.  
Documenting and sharing your work is a career skill. It compounds forever.

*Month 10*  
**The business of design.**  
Pricing. Positioning. Getting clients. Keeping them.  
The stuff no design school teaches and the stuff that actually determines your career trajectory.

**7 — CTA**

**Most design education teaches tools. We'd teach thinking.**

The tools change. The thinking doesn't.

*Like · Save · Share*

## Notes

**The cover is a thought experiment.** "If we ran a design school, this is what we'd teach" makes the reader the student and the author the dean. It sets a bar ("what's missing from every school") that every month on the map has to beat.

**The roadmap is drawn as a route, not listed.** A yellow dashed line winds across the slides from Month 1 to Month 10 on a blue grid like a blueprint. Swiping follows the line, which is the closest a carousel gets to a journey, and each month is a headline plus two or three lines in small type.

**Each month names the thing other schools skip.** "Most people start with tutorials. We'd start here." / "Every designer will touch this in 2026. Zero schools are teaching it properly yet." / "This gets zero time in most curriculums." The comparison is the argument, and it repeats in different words so the reader keeps score.

**The close is one sentence with a symmetry.** "Most design education teaches tools. We'd teach thinking. The tools change. The thinking doesn't." The final ask is the three platform icons, Like, Save, Share, with no comment word.
