## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
**Claude Carousel Blueprint**
*that generates massive engagement.*

**2**
**Most brands create carousels without thinking about what makes people save.**

They're designed. Posted. Ignored.
**We do something different.**

**3 — 1:**
*Claude defines* **the save moment**

Before we create anything, Claude answers **"Why would someone actually save this carousel?"** Save means they want to return. They see value they'll need later.

> Claude identifies what that value is. Likes are fleeting. Saves are commitments.

**4 — 2:**
*Claude builds the* **hook that stops the scroll**

The first slide determines if they swipe. Claude asks: What makes someone pause their feed right here?

*Three example covers shown:* **A question** · **A promise** · **A revelation.**

Claude generates hook variations until one creates genuine curiosity.

**5 — 3:**
*Claude Maps The* **Value Escalation**

- Slide 1: Hook that stops scroll.
- Slide 2: Problem that creates urgency.
- Slide 3–8: Value that builds momentum.
- Slide 9–11: Insight that feels earned.
- Slide 12: CTA that feels natural.

Every slide adds value. Every slide earns the next swipe. Carousels with flat structure get skipped. Carousels with escalating value get saved.

**6 — 4:**
*Claude defines the* **save worthy insight**

Halfway through the carousel, Claude places the insight. **The moment where readers go "oh, that's why."**

This is what people save for. Not the beginning. Not the end. **The middle where everything clicks.**

Claude identifies this insight before design. Before copy. **Saves happen when insights are earned, not delivered.**

**7 — 5:**
**Claude Optimises For Scroll Speed**
Claude knows how fast people scroll.

- Short sentences early. Longer paragraphs when they're invested.
- Visuals at moments where text fatigue hits.
- Pauses built in naturally.

**Carousels lose readers at scroll-fatigue moments. Claude builds breathing room.**

**8 — 6:**
*Claude designs the* **CTA that converts**

The final slide isn't "follow me" or "comment below." **Claude asks: What's the most natural next step?**

*Three examples:* **Save this carousel** · **Share with someone** · **Comment your version**

**The CTA matches the carousel's energy.** Wrong CTA tanks saves. Right CTA multiplies them.

**9**
**What Claude Actually Does**
Claude isn't writing the carousel. It's asking the questions most creators skip.

☐ Why would someone save this?
☐ What's the save-worthy moment?
☐ Where does scroll fatigue hit?
☐ What's the most natural CTA?

These questions determine the output. **Better questions create better carousels.**

**10 — CTA**
Comment ⭐ if you want more Claude tips like this!

## Notes

**Its structural model, stated outright on slide 5:** hook → problem → value that builds → insight that feels earned → natural CTA. That is the same spine as posts 04, 07 and 09, written down. Note it assumes a 12-slide carousel; the system's 4–9 range compresses the "Slide 3–8 value" band, not the shape.

**"Likes are fleeting. Saves are commitments."** The clearest one-line statement in the file of why a carousel is built differently from a reel — and a useful reframe for the goal: a save is a viewer telling you they intend to come back.

**"Saves happen when insights are earned, not delivered."** The strongest idea in the post, and it corroborates what post 11 does in practice: the meaning has to arrive after the work, not instead of it.

**It puts the big insight in the middle, not the end** — *"Not the beginning. Not the end. The middle where everything clicks."* Worth holding against post 01, where the pivot ("Thoughts are practice too") lands on slide 6 of 12 — exactly halfway. Two independent sources for the same placement.

**"Short sentences early. Longer paragraphs when they're invested."** A pacing rule with a reason: the reader's tolerance for text grows as they invest swipes. It explains why front-loading dense copy kills a post and why slide 7 can be twice the length of slide 2.

**Its own CTA contradicts its own advice.** Slide 8 says *"The final slide isn't 'follow me' or 'comment below'"* — and slide 10 is *"Comment ⭐ if you want more Claude tips like this!"*, a generic comment-below with no deliverable. Same weakness as post 14 from the same account. **The account can articulate the principle and does not apply it**, which is a useful reminder that a post explaining best practice is not evidence the practice was followed.

**Slide 9 is a checklist recap, and it earns its place** by converting the six preceding slides into four questions the reader can actually apply. A recap that reformats rather than repeats.

**Formatting habit worth stealing:** every teaching slide's title is a two-part line — italic serif clause in orange (*"Claude defines the"*) followed by bold sans in white (**"save worthy insight"**). Two typefaces, two colours, one sentence. It makes six differently-worded titles feel like one series.
