They think it's about confidence. It's not. It's meant to feel scary and uncomfortable.

At least a couple of times a week, I email or DM people I admire — professionally or personally. There's no reason for me to think this will work, but I do it anyway.

You know what they say...closed mouths don't get fed.

**4**

**01. pick your goals**

know what you're aiming for before reaching out.

• **get specific.** A job you want, a room you want in, a friend group, a skill you want to learn, or just more clarity on what your next step is.
• **keep the list short.** Choose 2–3 goals you want to work on right now to avoid analysis paralysis.
• **some examples:** creating a network in a new industry, understanding how someone made a career pivot, landing a role at a specific company, getting feedback.

*A filing cabinet, red lips and a sticker note on the photo.*

**5**

**02. choose your people**

divide them into three categories:

• **peers.** People in the same realm as you, doing similar work, at a similar stage.
• **mentors or clients.** More senior people a few steps ahead who could be the key to unlocking your next opportunity.
• **big shots.** The names that feel slightly delusional. Worth adding to your list even if the odds are lower.

LinkedIn is the best place to find the right people.

*Photo of a hand holding a vintage telephone receiver.*

**6**

**03. shoot the shot**

• **be thoughtful.** No AI slop or generic emails allowed! A well-crafted, thoughtful message is so much more likely to get a reply.
• **make one clear ask.** Don't bury it in context. Be clear and specific about what you want.
• **follow up.** Most people are busy. Follow up twice in two weeks, and after that, graciously let it be.

*Photo of a woman in a blazer holding a phone, a sticker reading* I don't want a mediocre life sorry

**7**

here's a little example

Hi [Name], I'm [your name]. I'm currently [what you're doing].

I've been following your work for a while and just wanted to say I really love [specific thing about their work], especially [something even more specific, a project, a decision, a piece of writing]. It's a genuine source of inspiration.

I'll be honest, I feel a little nervous writing this! But here's what I'm hoping for: [your ask, one sentence]

Would you be up for a quick coffee chat sometime? Or if easier, I can send over my questions via email. Totally get it if you're slammed — I want to be respectful of your time!

Thanks so much, either way!

[Your name]

**If you actually do this — write the list of dream people, dream opportunities, dream collaborators, and send the messages — your life looks completely different in six months.**

## Notes

**The cover is a promise to be practical and the first slide makes it a character.** "There's a specific kind of person who ends up with the best opportunities... It's never the most qualified person in the room. It's the one who's willing to look a little delusional." The reader is told what they would be if they act, before they are told how.

**The red-on-white magazine layout is the texture device.** Every slide is the same sans type, one bold red headline, one collaged photo and the handle in the footer, with a single full-red slide at the end for the sign-off. The reader reads a long how-to as a zine, not a lecture.

**The middle is a three-step method plus a script.** Pick goals, choose people, shoot the shot, with the example message on the next slide so nothing is left to guess. The line "we love a vulnerable diva" in the margin of the script is the permission to sound human.

**It ends on the fear, not on a link.** Slide 8 names the worst case ("they say no") and shrinks it to "over it by next week," then sets the upside against it. There is no comment word; the closing slide is the encouragement itself.
