## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**

3 Levels  
*of* BRANDING

*Three stone chess pieces: a bishop, a queen, a knight.*

Most brands never get past Level 1.

**2**

Every brand sits at one of three levels.

The gap between forgettable  
and worth fighting for?

Which level you're operating at?

*The three chess pieces labelled Level 1 (bishop), Level 3 (queen), Level 2 (knight).*

*Footer on every slide 2–12:* Save for later · Swipe →

**3**

Level 1:

**Random Pairing**

This is where  
96%  
of brands live.

— Posting without a plan  
— Showing up when you feel like it  
— Your audience can't explain what you do in one sentence

*A bishop breaking apart.*

**4**

**What Level 1 Looks Like**

01 Content with no recurring theme  
02 Visuals that don't match the message  
03 Collaborating with anyone who'll have you

If someone heard your name tomorrow,  
they'd have nothing to say about you.

**5**

Level 2:

**Intentional Pairing**

You pick the associations you want attached to your name.

Then you pair yourself with those things on purpose.

Nike  
→ athletic greatness

Harley-Davidson  
→ freedom & adventure

They didn't stumble into those associations.  
They chose them.

*A stone knight.*

**6**

**How to Get to Level 2**

Three questions. Work backwards from here:

What do I want to be known for?  
What do I have to do to earn that?  
What do I need to learn to pull it off?

*Every piece of content, every collab, every public move should reinforce the association you chose.*

**7**

**The Part Most People Skip**

Level 2 isn't only about what you pair yourself with.  
It's about what you refuse to pair yourself with.

If you keep showing up alongside people your audience doesn't trust, that association sticks to you.

*Every piece of content, every collab, every public move should reinforce the association you chose.*

*A knight crumbling.*

**8**

Level 3:

**Audience Ownership**

This is where everything changes.

Level 2  
They associate you with success stories.

Level 3  
They associate you with *their own* success.

*They consumed your content, took action, got results.*  
Now they believe anything they invest in you pays back more than they put in.

*A stone queen.*

**9**

**The Level 3 Flywheel**

This is where everything changes.

Share lessons + frameworks (free) → Audience trusts you (built through proof) → They take action and get results → They associate you with their wins → *(back to the start)*

When you finally make an offer, the conversation isn't "convince me." It's "take my money."

**10**

**Why Most Brands Stay Stuck**

Level 1 brands  
Post without intention.

Level 2 brands  
Pair intentionally, but never build enough trust to reach Level 3.

And the ones who try to skip straight to monetization without earning trust?

*They burn their audience and start over.*

You can't shortcut your way from forgettable to unforgettable.

**11**

**The Framework**

Level 1 — Random pairing. Unclear positioning. Inconsistent output. Audience doesn't stick around.  
↓  
Level 2 — Intentional pairing. Clear associations. Consistent execution. Trust starts building.  
↓  
Level 3 — Audience ownership. Your audience wins because of you. Trust becomes the product.

Each level builds on the one before it.  
Skip one and the whole thing collapses.

**12 — CTA**

Your brand already exists whether you define it or not.  
The only question is which level you're building at.

Looking to build a Level 3 personal brand?

Comment  
**"Level"**  
and let's see if we're a good fit.

## Notes

**The cover names a ladder and then tells the reader they are on the bottom rung.** "3 Levels of Branding" is a plain named model. "Most brands never get past Level 1" is the line that makes it personal: nobody reads that and assumes they are the exception, so they swipe to check.

**Slide 2 turns the model into a self-test before explaining anything.** "Which level you're operating at?" The levels are labelled on the chess pieces but not defined. The reader now has a question about themselves that only the next ten slides can answer.

**Each level gets the same two-slide card.** A name slide ("Level 1: Random Pairing", with what it is) followed by a detail slide ("What Level 1 Looks Like", "How to Get to Level 2", "The Level 3 Flywheel"). The repeat lets the reader predict the shape and read faster, and every heading is a label that makes sense alone.

**Level 1 is described so the reader recognises themselves.** "Posting without a plan / Showing up when you feel like it / Your audience can't explain what you do in one sentence", then "If someone heard your name tomorrow, they'd have nothing to say about you." The list is behaviour, the closing line is the cost. "96%" tells them they are in the majority, which makes it safe to admit.

**The examples are brands everyone already holds an association for.** "Nike → athletic greatness", "Harley-Davidson → freedom & adventure". The reader proves the idea to themselves because the association fires before they finish reading the line. "They didn't stumble into those associations. They chose them." is the X, not Y that makes it a method.

**"The Part Most People Skip" is an info gap in a heading.** It promises something the reader has missed, then delivers a reversal: "It's about what you refuse to pair yourself with."

**The Level 2 to Level 3 difference is one swapped phrase.** "They associate you with success stories." against "They associate you with their own success." Two short lines, three words apart, carrying the whole top of the model.

**The post recaps itself twice before the ask.** "Why Most Brands Stay Stuck" restates the levels as failure points, and "The Framework" restates them as one diagram with "Skip one and the whole thing collapses." For an abstract model this is the wrap-up that ties the items together and gives the reader a slide worth saving (every slide carries "Save for later").

**The CTA qualifies instead of giving.** "Looking to build a Level 3 personal brand? Comment "Level" and let's see if we're a good fit." The comment word comes from the post's own vocabulary, and the offer is a conversation about working together, not a freebie. The line before it, "Your brand already exists whether you define it or not", removes the option of doing nothing.

**Heavier slides than most of the file.** Roughly 30 to 60 words a slide with a heading on every body slide, bullets or numbered cards on most, and a diagram on two. A model post where the design carries structure that short-line argument posts carry with pacing.
