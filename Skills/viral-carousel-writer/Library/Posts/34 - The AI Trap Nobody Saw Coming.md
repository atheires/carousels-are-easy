## Notes

**The cover promises a conclusion, not a lesson.** *"My unexpected conclusion after teaching a 2-day Claude class."* The value is framed as something the author *discovered* by doing something specific, which is a different offer from "here's what I know". **A conclusion implies evidence the reader hasn't seen; a lesson implies advice they probably have.**

**Slide 2 leads with the number and then immediately gives it away to the audience.** *"More than 4,000 people joined"* → *"And first of all — I have SO much respect for you."* The credential is stated and then converted into praise for the reader within one slide. Three arrow bullets follow, all starting *"You keep…"* — the reader's persistence, not the author's reach.

**This is the cleanest handling of authority in the file.** Compare [[04 - How to Become a Claude Code Genius|post 04]], which shows a Stripe payout list. Both establish standing; this one spends it on the reader immediately, which buys permission for the criticism on slide 3.

**Slide 3 is the pivot and it is performed, not stated.** *"there is one thing I almost want to scream: Please STOP 😭 downloading random isolated 😭 AI tools 😭"* — capitals, italic display serif, three crying emojis interleaved *inside* the sentence. It reads as someone actually exasperated. Note the sequence: genuine warmth → *"but"* → outburst → the fix in small type (*"You need a foundation a.k.a. system"*).

**Slide 4 is the recognition slide and it works entirely through specific counts.** *20 saved AI tools · 10 browser tabs open · 5 paid subscriptions · 100 prompts saved somewhere.* Then: *"AND STILL… no clear system."*

Nobody has exactly those numbers, and everyone recognises the shape. **Inventing plausible specifics for the reader's own mess is more convincing than describing it accurately in general terms.** The chips are set at jaunty angles, visually cluttered — the layout performs the clutter it describes.

**Slide 13 is a screenshot of the author's own Notes app, laid over a photo of her recording.** The argument's most important line is delivered as if it were a private note rather than a slide:

> *"People are not confused because AI is too difficult. They're confused because there is **too much information and no architecture.**"*

**Presenting the thesis as a found artefact rather than a designed slide** makes it read as sincere. The highlight on *too much information and no architecture* is the same yellow-marker device a reader would use on their own notes.

**"Everyone is hyping tools. Very few people are showing you how to connect them into something that actually works."** The gap sentence, and it doubles as the positioning statement for the product sold on the next slide.

**The CTA asks for a *recording request*, not a download.** *"IF YOU WANT TO REQUEST THE RECORDINGS, COMMENT: RECORDING."* The word *request* is doing careful work — it implies the recordings are of a real event that already happened with 4,000 people in it, and that access is granted rather than automatic. Scarcity without a countdown.

**Every slide ends with a small arrow bottom-right, and slide 1 has a progress bar.** Cheap, constant swipe encouragement across fourteen slides.
