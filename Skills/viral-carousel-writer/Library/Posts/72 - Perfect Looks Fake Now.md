## Notes

**The cover is four words and a reversal of a compliment.** "Perfect / looks fake / now" takes the word every creator is working towards and makes it the accusation. "Now" is the word doing the work: it says this was not true before, which is exactly what slide 2 goes back to explain.

**Slides 2 and 3 build the old rule before breaking it.** "For years, polish meant trust" and "Perfect used to signal: quality / taste / effort / professionalism" spend two slides making the reader agree with the thing about to be taken away. The past tense ("meant", "used to") keeps the tension open across both slides without a question being asked.

**The turn is one line and one piece of evidence.** "Then AI made perfect cheap." The screenshot under it is a real artist having to defend hand-made work as not AI. The argument is not asserted, it is shown happening to someone, and "People can't tell anymore what's authentic" names what the reader just watched.

**Slide 5 gives the mechanism in plain cause and effect.** "And when perfect gets easier to make, it stops feeling special." This is the sentence the whole post rests on, and it arrives only after the evidence.

**"Too smooth. too clean. too generated." is three in a row where the last one costs.** The first two are things people used to want. The third reveals what they now mean.

**The answer is split over three slides.** "So what stands out now isn't perfection." / "It's presence." / the four examples. The negative gets its own slide, the one-word answer gets its own slide, and only then is it made concrete ("A real opinion. A weird detail. A lived story. A human point of view."). Each slide ends with the reader needing the next.

**The close swaps one word in a question.** "Don't ask: How do I make this look perfect?" then "Ask: How do I make this feel human?" Same sentence, "look perfect" against "feel human". It hands the reader a replacement habit without a list of steps.

**The CTA is a question, not an offer.** "What makes content feel human to you? Tell me in the comments." No keyword, no freebie. It reuses "feel human" from the slide before, so the ask is the reader answering the question they were just told to ask themselves.

**Twelve slides, none over about fifteen words.** Same build as [[38 - Stop Sharing How Much Money You Make Online]] by the same account: one thought per slide, serif statement plus a handwritten aside, an Argument with no method.
