## Notes

**The hook is a confession, and it is selling.** *"I can't keep this to myself anymore."* Framed as an emotional overflow — something the author can no longer contain — when the post is a waitlist drive. **The most commercial post in the file has the least commercial-sounding cover.**

**The subhead does the real work:** *"Five weeks ago, I launched my first coaching program. And it became something completely different from what I expected."* A time-box, a specific event, and an unresolved surprise. The reader swipes to find out what changed, not to hear about a program.

**Slide 2 tells a story about being wrong.** *"Initially, I thought I was creating a program about AI avatars. But after working closely with my students, I realized: a beautiful AI avatar is no longer enough."*

Admitting the original premise was insufficient is what makes the expanded offer credible. **A pivot narrated as a discovery sells a bigger product without ever claiming to sell one.** The speech bubbles and hand-drawn arrow make it read as thinking rather than positioning.

**Slide 3 is the proof slide and the statistic is chosen with unusual care.** *"60% of our students had already created AI avatars before joining."*

That number argues that the *obvious* solution is not the solution — the customers had already done the thing everyone sells and still needed help. **A statistic about what the audience already tried is more persuasive than a statistic about results.** Then the wall, then the four missing pieces, then *"It just doesn't work."*

**The curriculum is drawn as a winding path, not a list.** Five week-labels on a meandering green line. A list of five modules reads as a syllabus; a path reads as a journey with an end. Cheap conversion of information into desire.

**Slide 13 thanks the existing students before asking anything.** *"Thank you, my dears, for all the love you've shown this program. It truly means more to me than I can explain."* Placed immediately before the CTA — the same **gratitude-then-ask** placement as [[12 - If Claude Writes Your Content|post 12]]'s empathy slide at 11 of 12. It also functions as social proof: there is love to be thanked for.

**The CTA is the strongest offer-stack in the file.**

> *"The next cohort is coming soon. IF YOU MISSED THE LAST ONE, COMMENT WAITLIST TO GET: Up to 50% off · Exclusive bonuses · The best available offer."*

Four things worth noting:

1. **"If you missed the last one"** presumes a previous sold-out cohort and makes the reader late rather than early.
2. **The comment word buys a price, not a file.** Every other funnel here trades a word for a PDF; this trades it for a discount and a queue position.
3. **Three escalating benefits**, ending on the unfalsifiable *"the best available offer"* — which removes the reader's fear of a better deal appearing later.
4. **No deadline is stated.** *Soon* plus a waitlist creates urgency without a date the author has to honour.

**The whole post is a case study of its own product, told as a diary entry.** Slides 1–3 are reflection, 4 is the offer, 13 is gratitude, 14 is the ask. Nothing is taught. **When the product is a cohort, the carousel's job is to prove the room is worth being in — not to give value away.** Worth holding against the comment-word posts (03–09) where the deliverable is a file: different offer, different obligation.
