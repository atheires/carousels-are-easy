## Notes

**The cover borrows a famous name and a big number.** "Jessi Jean's $7.4 million Yap Challenge" is a client's result, and the hand-lettered "GOAT" with an arrow shows who the author admires. The reader is invited to look behind the scenes at something they already saw succeed.

**It is a case study told as a text thread.** The voice-note bar on slide 2 makes it feel like the client messaged the author. Each slide is a sentence or two in lowercase handwriting with yellow highlights on the clause that matters: "didn't feel confident that her automations were firing properly", "didn't want there to be any friction".

**Problem, fix, proof.** The problem has a number (10,000 people on the waitlist) and a fear (automations might not be firing). The fix takes two slides and one diagram. The proof arrives after a one-line beat, "and, well....it worked...", and then gives two screenshots: $411,293 and 22,043 emails.

**The pitch is the only ask and it is plain.** "We have 3 spots open for september to work 1:1 with you :)" is a limit, and "comment 'APPLY' and i'll send you the application to see if it's a good fit!" frames it as a mutual fit check, not a sale. Compare [[73 - 3 Levels of Branding]] for the same "comment and see if we're a good fit" close.
