## Notes

**The cover promises ten and the post delivers three.** The subhead says *"Here Are The 10 Things They All Have"*; only "the final three" ever appear. Whether that is a deliberate tease or a broken post, the effect on a reader is the same — **a headline number the body does not honour is the fastest way to lose trust**, and it is the clearest counter-example in this file to the numbered-list posts that do pay off (03, 08, 09, 10).

**Slide 2 is a well-built promise slide even so.** Assumption → correction → escalation → hook: *"think viral reels are random"* / *"but there's a pattern"* / *"Not one element. Ten."* / *"Once you see the pattern, you can't unsee it."* Four beats in four short lines. The two-word fragment *"Not one element. Ten."* is the strongest thing in the post.

**"Once you see it, you can't unsee it"** is a reusable curiosity device — it promises a permanent change in perception rather than a piece of information, which is a bigger claim at the same cost.

**The CTA is the weakest in the file.** *"Comment 🎬 if you want more posts like this."* No deliverable, no name, no reason. Compare posts 03/08/09: comment a word, get a specific named artefact. This asks the reader to do work for the account's benefit and offers nothing back — which is why it is worth keeping as a negative example.

**The cover image is unrelated to the content.** A swimwear photo on a post about reel structure. It buys the stop and then contributes nothing to the argument. Contrast post 08, where the cover art *was* the argument.
