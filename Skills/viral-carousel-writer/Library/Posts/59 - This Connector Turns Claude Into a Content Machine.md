## Notes

**The cover sells a transformation and names its scope in one glance.** *“THIS CONNECTOR TURNS CLAUDE INTO a content machine”* is a compact before-and-after claim, while the four labels—*“YOUR BRAND STRATEGY / CALENDAR / DRAFTS / CAMPAIGNS”*—make “machine” concrete. The small *“managed through chat”* line adds the interaction model; it answers how the promise works before the reader has to ask.

**Slide 2 trades suspense for setup speed.** *“CONNECT VOCABLE IN ABOUT 90 SECONDS”* follows the outcome with a low-friction proof of access. The numbered directions are deliberately literal—*“Customize → Connectors → Add custom connector”*—so the carousel begins delivering practical value immediately after its claim.

**Slide 3 explains why this connector matters before walking through its use.** *“Connectors give it tools to execute”* bridges the general premise to Vocable’s concrete abilities, and the longer sentence names the work it can handle. The final contrast—*“less time running the content process”* and *“more time reviewing work”*—turns features into a change in the reader’s day.

**Slides 4–7 make the process feel cumulative by keeping each step distinct.** Slide 4 asks Claude to establish *“my positioning, content strategy, and voice”*; slide 5 then carries that context into *“one shared objective”* across channels. That setup makes slide 6’s request to create the campaign feel like the next logical action, and slide 7 closes the loop by moving approved work into *“DISTRIBUTION.”*

**The repeated quoted commands make the carousel usable as an operating script.** *“Use Vocable to build my brand intelligence”* and *“Write all the drafts inside Vocable using my brand intelligence”* use first-person language the reader can borrow directly. The commands also keep the explanation grounded in actual inputs and outputs rather than abstract promises about AI.

**The sentence lengths alternate between instruction and payoff.** Slide 4 gives a dense prompt, then eases into *“You don’t need to set anything up inside Vocable first.”* Slide 6 follows another long prompt with the broader payoff: *“One message can become several useful pieces.”* Those shorter lines let the reader absorb the benefit between procedural details.

**The CTA is earned by the demonstrated workflow.** *“One conversation can carry your content from business context to execution”* restates the whole sequence in one line, then *“Comment MCP for my free Guide”* gives a direct next step. The offer is relevant because the preceding slides have shown the connection, the prompts, and the publishing path.

**This is a Build carousel with a light product argument.** The four labeled steps are the spine, while *“Claude is smart. Connectors give it tools to execute”* supplies the argument for why the build is useful. The post stays focused on showing an end-to-end workflow rather than becoming a broad inventory of connector features.
