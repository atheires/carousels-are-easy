## Notes

**The hook is the strongest structural device in this file.** *"Hacks I'd gatekeep if I wanted ADHD entrepreneurs to fail."*

It is a **negative-frame list**. Instead of "8 hacks for ADHD entrepreneurs" — which is information, and would be skipped — it implies (a) these are good enough that withholding them would be sabotage, (b) other people *are* withholding them, and (c) the writer is choosing not to. Three claims in one sentence, none of them stated.

**It converts a generic listicle into a generous act.** This is the single most transferable line-shape in the file, and it works for any list:

> *"Things I'd keep quiet about if I wanted [audience] to stay stuck."*

**The audience is named in the hook.** *"ADHD entrepreneurs"* — so the filtering happens on slide 1 without narrowing the topic. Anyone with ADHD who runs anything stops. This is exactly the vault's own principle: keep the topic wide, narrow in the wording, not the subject.

**Every hack has a name and a mechanism.** *"magic 5 to-do list"* is a name you can repeat; the body says exactly what to do and what you get — *"6 tasks done in 30 minutes plus dopamine to kick start your day."* Naming a tactic makes it feel like a possession rather than advice.

**The reason is always ADHD-specific.** *"Less tools to manage = less executive funtion."* / *"ADHD brains justify perfecitonism as productivity in the last 20%."* The same eight hacks written for a general audience would be a generic productivity list. **The mechanism is what makes a common tactic feel written for one person.**

**One photograph on all ten slides, with the headline repeated small at the bottom of every slide.** Zero design cost, and the repeated headline means any single slide screenshot carries the hook. Note this is the Inventory pattern again — total layout repetition across a parallel set.

**Typos left in** — *perfecitonism*, *funtion* — as in [[12 - If Claude Writes Your Content|post 12]]. On an ADHD account this reads as consistent rather than careless.

**The last slide is an introduction, not an ask.** *"Hi I'm Ari, I help ADHD entrepreneurs build businesses that work with their brains."* The reader has just received eight useful things and only then finds out who was talking. The bracketed *"[and i'm really f***ing good at it]"* is the whole voice in one line — the claim would be arrogant in plain type, and the brackets make it a joke that still lands as a credential.

**The CTA is a follow, and it is written in the reader's language:** *"hit follow to build TF out of your ADHD owned business."* Weaker than a comment-word funnel for lead capture, but consistent with a personal-brand account.
