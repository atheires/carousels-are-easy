# 66 — How to Be Obscenely Consistent

**Account:** @tinnaloaizaofficial
**Slides:** 10

## Slide copy

**1 — cover**
**How to Be Obscenely Consistent.**
*EVEN IF YOU’VE FALLEN OFF BEFORE.*

**2**
**I think we made consistency WAY too dependent on never f*cking up.**

Miss three days and suddenly we need a comeback plan.

Miss two weeks and apparently we are “completely starting from scratch”.

Mate, why?

Your life interrupted you, and it is probably going to do that again.

If every interruption sends the whole thing back to zero, **I’m starting to think the interruption is not actually the main issue.**

**3**
**The people I think are ridiculously consistent still disappear sometimes.**

They just don’t turn returning into a ceremonial event.

No new planner, “I’ll do it on Monday” and most definitely no “this month I’m finally going to be disciplined”.

**They just... make the next thing.**

Which sounds annoyingly simple until you realise how much energy we waste waiting to feel properly BACK.

**4**
**I used to be terrible at this.**

If I planned five posts and managed two, somehow my ADHD brain did not register TWO POSTS.

It registered... she failed the plan.

Which makes absolutely no sense.

Two posts existed. **Zero would have been objectively worse.**

But zero came with the very attractive fantasy that next week I would execute everything perfectly, apparently as a completely different woman.

**5**
**And yes, sometimes the plan needs to get smaller.**

This one took me embarrassingly long.

If the normal week has room for five posts and this week only has room for two... **then maybe we post two.**

Wild, I know.

For a long time I treated the smaller version like it somehow didn’t count, which is how five impossible posts became zero surprisingly often.

**6**
**Some parts of your process should survive a crap week without you.**

I want the template to still exist.

I want my ideas to still be sitting somewhere instead of relying on me remembering the brilliant thought I had while driving three days ago.

And I want to know the handful of conversations I can always come back to without Wednesday Tinna having to rebuild the entire content department because Monday and Tuesday went non verbal.

**That poor woman has enough going on.**

**7**
**Now I care a lot more about what happens when I come back.**

Do I roughly know what I’m posting, or am I opening Instagram and starting the whole decision-making process again?

Have I already decided the formats?

Is there somewhere to pull an idea from, or am I meant to become fascinating on command after a week where everything went to sh*t?

Because THAT is usually the bit that **makes returning feel heavier than it needs to.**

**8**
**Maybe consistency is less about never stopping than we make it out to be.**

I’m much more interested now in whether the thing still knows what to do AFTER I stop.

**Because I will. You will.** Something unexpected is bound to happen.

I’d rather build around that reality than keep designing a plan for a woman who apparently never gets tired, distracted, sick or interrupted.

That woman is probably doing 5am cold plunges and journaling straight after in a parallel reality.

**9**
**So if you keep “falling off”, I’d look at what you are actually coming back TO.**

Because if every return means deciding the formats again, finding ideas again, figuring out what the hell you even talk about again and somehow matching all of that to whatever capacity you have left that week...

of course coming back feels heavy.

I don’t think the answer is getting better at forcing yourself through that.

I think **there should just be a lot less to rebuild.**

**10 — CTA**
**That is basically the whole reason I built The Content Engine.**

I wanted a system where I already knew what I talk about, where ideas come from, which formats I can actually sustain and what each piece of content is meant to do for the business.

So instead of starting from scratch every Monday, you build the repeatable parts once and then keep pulling from them.

If you want a content system you can actually keep using when life gets messy, **comment “MELON” and I’ll send you the details.**

## Notes

**The cover promises consistency, then quietly loosens its usual definition.** *“How to Be Obscenely Consistent. EVEN IF YOU’VE FALLEN OFF BEFORE.”* The first line sounds like a familiar discipline pitch; the subtitle addresses people who have already broken their streak, so the tension is whether consistency can include interruption.

**Slide 2 turns a common setback into the argument’s target.** *“I think we made consistency WAY too dependent on never f*cking up.”* The examples of three missed days and two missed weeks make the all-or-nothing rule feel recognizable before the sharper ending says the interruption may not be the main issue. It redirects blame without yet giving a solution.

**Slide 3 supplies a compact counterexample instead of a lecture.** *“They just... make the next thing.”* The people described as consistent still disappear, so the distinction is their return ritual, not perfect attendance. Its short, plain phrase lands after the elaborate planner and Monday-reset scenarios, making the behavior feel almost embarrassingly simple.

**Slide 4 makes the cost of perfection personal and measurable.** *“Two posts existed. Zero would have been objectively worse.”* The five-versus-two plan gives the self-judgment a concrete object, then exposes the absurd comparison with zero. The admission about ADHD lets the reader see the mechanism at work rather than receive generic encouragement.

**Slide 5 changes the unit of success from the ideal week to the available week.** *“If the normal week has room for five posts and this week only has room for two... then maybe we post two.”* “Wild, I know” carries the speaker’s old resistance into the reader’s likely reaction; the next sentence shows how rejecting a smaller version can turn five impossible posts into none.

**Slides 6–7 locate the practical friction in rebuilding after a gap.** *“Some parts of your process should survive a crap week without you”* names a system that can hold templates, ideas, and recurring conversations; the following questions test whether those pieces are ready when she returns. The jokes about “Wednesday Tinna” and becoming “fascinating on command” keep the operational point rooted in an actual week.

**Slides 8–9 finish the redefinition before naming the product.** *“whether the thing still knows what to do AFTER I stop”* becomes the standard, and *“there should just be a lot less to rebuild”* turns that standard into a clear diagnosis. The cause-and-effect chain makes the system feel like a response to the problem already demonstrated, not a sudden sales detour.

**The CTA earns its offer by echoing the mechanism the carousel has built.** *“build the repeatable parts once and then keep pulling from them”* answers the repeated return-to-zero problem, while the comment keyword gives a specific next action. The genre is **Argument / Build**: it challenges a rigid belief about consistency, then points toward a repeatable content process.
