## Notes

**The hook threatens the reader's standard of success, not the existence of marketing.** “The era of good marketing is over.” makes “good” the endangered category. A reader who has worked to produce competent copy has a reason to keep reading: the post appears to withdraw the value of that achievement. The later slides must therefore distinguish lost advantage from uselessness, and they do.

**Slide 2 grants that the old effort once paid off.** “Five years ago, all of these gave you an edge” follows the provocative cover with recognition. “a polished website,” “a well-written caption” and “a decent sales page” name the work the reader has actually done. The temporal qualification prevents the cover from simply insulting that work; it creates a before-state whose change now needs explaining.

**The main insight lands as a change in relative value.** “Then AI happened” is the short hinge between the old advantage and “Good became the baseline.” The next sentence supplies the mechanism: “when everyone has access to \"good,” good stops being memorable.” Repeating “good” makes the same word lose its status in front of the reader. The argument concerns what distinguishes a brand, not whether a sentence is grammatically competent.

**The first question converts a loss into a map of new advantages.** “So what’s happening now?” comes after the baseline explanation, when the reader needs an alternative. The answers move from audience resistance—“a new filter for AI slop”—to things worth paying for: originality, taste and human stories. “premium” and “moat” translate personal expression into commercial value rather than leaving it as a plea to be authentic.

**The second question makes the reader test authorship rather than polish.** “CAN YOU CREATE GOOD CONTENT?” becomes “CAN YOU CREATE SOMETHING ONLY YOU COULD'VE WRITTEN?” after the list has established why originality matters. The repeated opening makes the changed object unmistakable. “ONLY YOU,” including the underline on “YOU,” is the sharpest demand in the post: another round of editing cannot by itself satisfy it.

**The starting-line correction turns the cover's alarm into a higher threshold.** “Good is now the finish starting line,” with “finish” crossed out, visibly revises the reader's definition of completion. It comes after the authorship test because the new standard is now concrete enough to accept. “Which means your standards have to change too. :)” brings the market argument home, with the smile softening an instruction that could otherwise feel scolding.

**The CTA offers exactly the distinction the questions have made valuable.** “Let's write copy that's impossible to mistake for anyone else's” answers “ONLY YOU COULD'VE WRITTEN” in service language. “Ready to raise the bar for your brand?” carries slide 6's metaphor into a personal decision. The website, sales page and email labels return to the assets on slides 2–3, now sold under a changed standard; “DM ME ‘COPY’” is the practical next step.

**This is an Argument about a moving baseline.** “all of these gave you an edge” → “Good became the baseline” → “the question has changed” is a causal progression, not an inventory of AI complaints. The force comes from conceding yesterday's competence before asking for today's distinctiveness. The CTA depends on the reader accepting that particular shift.
