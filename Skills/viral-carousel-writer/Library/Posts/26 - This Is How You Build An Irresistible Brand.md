# 26 — This Is How You Build An Irresistible Brand

**Slides:** 7

Same studio and same structure as [[23 - The Anatomy of Great Packaging|post 23]] — an argument laid over a portfolio, closing with "Apply through the link in bio."

## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
**This is how you build an *irresistible* brand.**
*Extreme close-up of a mouth with red lips beside a salt-rimmed cocktail.*

**2**
Most brands try to be liked.

**The irresistible ones are built to be wanted.**

Liked gets ignored. Wanted gets chosen.

**3**
**→ Start here:**

What do people want to feel when they choose you?

Smarter?
Safer?
Ahead of everyone else?

Your brand should be built around that feeling, not your logo.

**4**
**Irresistible brands aren't louder.**

They're clearer.
Clear on who they're for.
Clear on who they're not for.
Clear on what they stand for when it's inconvenient.

**5**
Look at the brands people *can't stop talking about:*

They have a point of view.
They repeat it everywhere.
Website. Product. Hiring. Packaging. Founder's voice.

Consistency is what turns *attention into desire.*

**6**
**People don't want more options.**

**They want the one that already knows what they need.**

**7 — CTA**
Want your brand to be the obvious choice, not one of many? Let's build that.

**→ Apply through the link in bio.**

## Notes

**Every slide is one X-not-Y reversal.** Liked/wanted. Louder/clearer. More options/the one that knows. The post has no steps, no numbers and no framework — it is six consecutive reframes, and each one is small enough to remember.

**"Liked gets ignored. Wanted gets chosen."** Identical construction to [[23 - The Anatomy of Great Packaging|post 23]]'s *"Looks nice gets picked up. Feels right gets bought."* Same studio, same signature move: **two three-word clauses, one verb apart, stating the entire thesis.** This is a house style worth naming — a *paired-verdict line*. It is the most quotable shape in the whole file and it appears in the two strongest argument posts here and in [[22 - Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable|post 22]] (*"Once is an anecdote. Every time is a belief."*).

**Slide 3 hands the reader a question and three candidate answers** — *Smarter? Safer? Ahead of everyone else?* — exactly as post 23's slide 3 did with *Premium? Trustworthy? Different?* Then the same kind of corrective: *"Your brand should be built around that feeling, not your logo."*

**Slide 4's third clause is the one that does the work.** *"Clear on who they're for. Clear on who they're not for. Clear on what they stand for when it's inconvenient."* The first two are standard positioning advice. The third — *when it's inconvenient* — is the cost, and it converts a platitude into a standard the reader might actually fail. **Three parallel clauses where the last one raises the price.**

**Slide 5 lists where consistency has to live:** *"Website. Product. Hiring. Packaging. Founder's voice."* Hiring is the surprising item. Including one entry nobody expects is what stops a list reading as boilerplate.

**"Consistency is what turns attention into desire."** The mechanism sentence — it explains *why* the repetition on slide 5 matters, in the vocabulary the cover established.

**Slide 6 is the thesis alone on the slide, largest type in the post, over the most striking image.** Exactly the same placement decision as post 23's slide 6. **This studio always gives the single best line its own frame with no supporting copy.**

**The imagery is all desire, none of it is brand work.** Red lips and a salt rim, a perfume bottle out of focus, a leopard in a Cartier headband, a bare shoulder under blue light. A post about wanting things is illustrated with wanting, not with logos. Compare post 23, where the imagery was literal client work — the register shifts to match whether the subject is a craft or a feeling.

**Same CTA as post 23, word for word in structure:** a question naming the outcome, *"Let's build that"*, then *"→ Apply through the link in bio."* The word **Apply** again. For a studio taking projects, qualification beats volume — the opposite trade to the comment-word funnels in posts 03–09.
