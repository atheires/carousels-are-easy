03  
Carousels

A great carousel should feel like a story.

Use this structure:  
Hook → Value → Examples → More value → CTA

Keep every slide:  
• Simple  
• Scannable  
• Focused on ONE idea

Don't waste the last slide. Use it to summarize, create curiosity, or drive the next action.

*Sample carousel cover: "7 Video Editors you must Follow".*

04  
Viral Images

Viral LinkedIn images don't need complicated designs. They need one strong idea.

Use:

1. Short, powerful statement
2. Bold, readable typography
3. Simple background
4. Strong contrast
5. One clear visual concept

Your viewer should understand the message within seconds.

*Sample: NVIDIA founder image.*

**8**

The Viral Design Formula

STOP — UNDERSTAND — CONSUME — SHARE

Stop: Create a scroll-stopping headline.  
Understand: Make the message instantly clear.  
Consume: Use hierarchy, spacing and visuals to make reading effortless.  
Share: Give people something useful, relatable or surprising enough to pass on.

## Notes

**The cover stacks three proofs.** A face, "100M+ impressions using Infographics" and "+FREE Templates at the end". The reader knows the author, the credential and the reward before swiping, so the whole post reads as a lead-in to the freebie.

**One shape, four times.** Slides 4 to 7 each carry a number, a name, one sentence of advice and a short list or chain, plus a real sample from the author's own work. The reader can predict the layout from slide 4 onward, so the slides read fast and the samples do the proving.

**The wrap-up is a four-step formula in a table.** "Stop → Understand → Consume → Share" restates the four formats as what any good visual must do. It is the slide a reader would screenshot.

**The CTA is a two-part stack.** Slide 9 shows a free full course, slide 10 shows free templates, then one comment word collects both: "Comment - 'Infographic' & I'll send link of both to you". The author says the offers are free before asking for anything, and the "+FREE Templates at the end" on the cover pays off here.
