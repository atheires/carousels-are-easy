## Notes

**The hook makes the endangered thing your humanity, not your job.** “HOW TO STAY HUMAN / IN THE AGE OF AI” does not promise better prompts or protection from redundancy. “Stay” assumes the reader already possesses something worth preserving. That makes the instructions feel like acts of maintenance rather than a race to acquire another technical skill.

**Slide 2 answers an enormous anxiety with one deliberately modest act.** “READ SOMETHING / LONG” follows the civilisational scale of “THE AGE OF AI.” The drop in scale matters: the reader is given something they can actually do. “Something” removes the need to choose the correct prestigious book; “LONG” identifies the capacity being protected as sustained attention, not possession of the right information.

**“BADLY” turns making into resistance to effortless polish.** “MAKE SOMETHING / BADLY” comes after “READ SOMETHING / LONG,” carrying over “SOMETHING” but changing reception into production. The last word is the surprise. Making well could still sound like keeping up with a machine; making badly gives the reader permission to retain the awkward human experience that automated output lets them bypass.

**The middle moves from mediated contact to shared presence.** “CALL INSTEAD / OF TEXT” is followed by “EAT WITH PEOPLE.” First the reader replaces written messages with a voice; then the post removes the communication interface altogether. There is no literal question chain here. The implied question keeps becoming more concrete: what have you shortened, automated, or substituted for being with someone?

**Getting lost and being bored make inconvenience sound worth choosing.** “GET LOST / WITHOUT GPS” precedes “BE BORED FOR A WHILE.” One withholds an immediate answer about where to go; the next withholds immediate stimulation. “FOR A WHILE” is a small but important mercy: the reader is being invited to tolerate an interval, not abandon modern life. The list broadens from social connection into the ability to remain inside uncertainty.

**The phone-number pair gives leaving the device behind a small preparation.** “REMEMBER A / PHONE NUMBER” comes directly before “LEAVE YOUR PHONE / AT HOME.” The first recovers a tiny function people have handed to a device; the second asks them to loosen their dependence on the device itself. The sequence gives the stronger request a foothold in an ordinary, measurable act of memory.

**The ending expands from habits into a principle of living.** “FALL IN LOVE” and “TRUST YOUR TASTE” follow the practical phone instructions, moving into emotional exposure and personal judgment. Then “TAKE THE LONG WAY” gathers the earlier “LONG,” “BADLY,” “GET LOST,” and “BE BORED” into one final idea: the experience that a shortcut removes may be the thing worth having. That is where the inventory's underlying argument finally lands.

**This is an Inventory whose last instruction does the work of a conclusion.** “TAKE THE LONG WAY” asks for a change in behaviour rather than a comment, save, or purchase. The compact commands keep returning the reader to action, with no explanatory paragraph to consume in place of doing it. The absence of an engagement CTA is especially fitting after “LEAVE YOUR PHONE / AT HOME”: the post ends by sending the reader into life rather than asking for one more platform interaction.
