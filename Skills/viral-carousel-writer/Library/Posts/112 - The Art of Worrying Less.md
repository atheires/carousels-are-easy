## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**

==The Art of Worrying Less==

How to stop overthinking, trust yourself, and find peace in the present.

*Faded page of book text behind every slide. Titles on a lime highlight.*

**2**

==1. Most Things Are Not As Bad As You Imagine==

Your mind can turn a small problem into a hundred possible outcomes.

You imagine what could go wrong, what people might think, and what might happen next.

But most of those things never happen.

Do not let an imagined future disturb your present peace.

**3**

==2. You Do Not Need To Have Everything Figured Out==

It is normal to be uncertain about what comes next.

You do not need to know exactly where your life is going. Sometimes clarity comes after you take the next step.

Allow yourself to figure things out slowly.

**4**

==3. Deal With Problems When They Actually Happen==

You do not need to solve tomorrow's problems today.

Some things will resolve themselves. Some things will become clearer with time.

And some things simply are not worth worrying about.

Trust yourself enough to handle life as it comes. If something happens, you will deal with it then.

**5**

==4. Focus On What You Can Control==

You cannot control what other people think. You cannot control what has already happened.

You cannot control everything that happens tomorrow.

But you can control how you respond today. That is where your peace begins.

**6**

==5. Not Every Thought Deserves Your Attention==

Just because a thought enters your mind does not mean it is true.

Your mind will sometimes create fears, doubts, and worst-case scenarios.

You do not have to believe everything you think. Some thoughts are meant to pass.

Let them.

**7**

==6. Life Will Always Have Uncertainty==

You will never know exactly how everything will turn out.

And that is okay.

You do not need to know the entire path. You only need to take the next step.

**8**

As Mark Twain wrote,

**"I have had many troubles in my life, most of which never happened."**

Do not spend today worrying about a tomorrow that may never come.

That's it.

## Notes

**The cover gives three verbs.** "Stop overthinking, trust yourself, and find peace in the present." The reader knows what they will get and the three promises map onto the six points that follow: overthinking (1 and 5), trust (2 and 3), present (4 and 6).

**Each point is a heading and four short paragraphs, nothing else.** There are no images, no icons and no colour but the lime highlight on the heading. At about 45 words a slide it is the most text-led post in the file, and it stays readable because every paragraph is one or two sentences and the headings can be read alone.

**The lines are plain and gentle, never urgent.** "That is okay." "Let them." "Allow yourself to figure things out slowly." The post reads like advice a friend gives in the evening, which is why it suits a book-themed account and gets saved.

**It ends on a quote and four words.** The Mark Twain line, "I have had many troubles in my life, most of which never happened", is the whole argument of the post in one sentence, and "That's it." closes it. There is no CTA slide. Compare [[71 - The Art of Taking Nothing Personally]] from the same account.
