## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**

You're looking for content ideas in the wrong place.

(And it's probably not where you think.)

Let me explain...

*Photo of a man at two monitors in a dim room, a lamp glowing. Footer on every slide 2–7:* YOU'RE LOOKING FOR CONTENT IDEAS IN THE WRONG PLACE · page number 02–07

**2 — Why you keep running out of ideas.**

Most 9-5ers build content backwards.

They open Instagram, look at what everyone else is posting, then ask:

"What should I talk about?"

So eventually everything starts sounding the same.

That's because you're just searching outside yourself before looking at what you already know.

Here's what I mean by that...

*A grid of eight of the author's own covers.*

**3 — Stop collecting topics. Start collecting lessons.**

Topics are everywhere.

**Perspective is rare.**

You can make another post about productivity, career growth, leadership, or personal branding...

But what makes someone remember you is what your experiences taught you about those topics.

Ask yourself:

→ What did I believe 3 years ago that I disagree with now?  
→ What lesson took me way too long to learn?  
→ What advice would I give my younger self?

Those answers are harder to copy.

That's the beginning of you actually standing out.

**4 — Turn your experience into genuine value.**

Your story alone isn't valuable to your audience.

What they can take from it is.

Instead of: "Here's something that happened to me."

Think: "Here's what happened to me, what it taught me that could help you."

**The experience belongs to you. The lesson should belong to them.**

That's why strong personal brands can tell the same stories repeatedly without becoming repetitive.

The story stays similar, but the lesson changes.

*A cover reading "You need to hear this", 716K views.*

**5 — Your opinions are part of the content.**

If all you do is teach information people can Google, you become easy to replace.

Your audience also needs to know:

• what you believe  
• what you disagree with  
• what you would do differently  
• what you think people misunderstand  
• what standards you refuse to lower

You don't need controversial opinions just to get attention.

**You need clear opinions that reveal how you think.**

Information makes you useful. A point of view makes you memorable.

**6 — Let your audience tell you what to repeat.**

You don't need a brand new idea every day.

**Your comments, DMs, saves, shares, and conversations are already telling you what deserves more attention.**

If people keep responding to the same:

problem, story, belief, question, or desired outcome...

*that's a signal.*

Strong creators don't abandon a message because they already posted about it once.

They find new ways to say what their audience clearly needs to hear again.

**Repetition builds recognition.**

## Notes

**The cover says the answer is not where the reader thinks, and then says "Let me explain...".** The three lines together are a contract: a claim, a teaser, a promise to go slowly. The reader who has run out of ideas knows exactly which place they have been looking.

**The first body slide names the mistake with a picture of it.** "Most 9-5ers build content backwards. They open Instagram, look at what everyone else is posting, then ask: 'What should I talk about?'" gives the reader's own habit in the second person, in the order they do it. "That's because you're just searching outside yourself" is the diagnosis.

**Each slide is a bold headline on the left and the body on the right.** Two columns of small light type on a dark photo, with bold used for the one sentence per slide that carries the idea ("Perspective is rare", "The experience belongs to you. The lesson should belong to them.", "Repetition builds recognition."). It is the densest slide format in the file after the book-page posts.

**The three questions in slide 3 are the method.** "What did I believe 3 years ago that I disagree with now? What lesson took me way too long to learn? What advice would I give my younger self?" The reader can use them the same night, so the post delivers before the ask.

**The CTA is a new video lesson.** "Comment 'BASE' and I'll send my brand new video lesson revealing how to build a personal brand from scratch that actually stands out." The word is a base, the thing the post told the reader to build from. Compare [[88 - The One Line That Makes Me $200,000 a Month]] and [[41 - How To Know What Content To Make]] for the same topic.
