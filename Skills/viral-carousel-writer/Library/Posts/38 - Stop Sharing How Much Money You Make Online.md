## Notes

**The cover refuses the proof most online sellers lead with.** “Stop sharing / how much / money you / make online” attacks income disclosure as a persuasion habit. “I don’t give a f*ck” supplies an impatient audience voice underneath it. The profanity makes this sound like accumulated irritation rather than a careful marketing position; the reader is invited to recognise exhaustion before hearing an alternative.

**Slide 2 turns the attack into a shared burden.** “Share your income. / Get on camera. / Show the dream life.” names three demands before “I’m finding / the internet / exhausting lately.” After the aggressive “Stop,” those short imperatives sound like the advice the writer is tired of receiving. “I’m finding” then locates the complaint in her experience, giving the reader room to agree without first accepting a universal verdict on everyone who shares earnings.

**“My life into evidence” is the line that gives the irritation a reason.** “I don’t want to share / my earnings or turn / my life into evidence / that you should buy / something from me” follows the exhaustion slide. It explains what is exhausting: ordinary life is being recruited into a sales argument. “Evidence” is sharper than “content” here because it identifies the burden of proving commercial worth through personal display.

**The main insight lands as an alternative source of trust.** “I want to build / trust by showing / you how I think / and what goes / into my work” comes immediately after refusing earnings as evidence. Without this line the post could remain a complaint. “How I think” substitutes access to judgment for access to lifestyle, and “what goes / into my work” promises something a prospective buyer can inspect rather than envy.

**The concession is late enough to protect the position and early enough to prevent contempt.** “I understand / wanting to see / what’s possible. / I look for that too.” arrives after the writer has stated her own standard. It concedes the legitimate need underneath aspirational marketing without retracting the objection to earnings theatre. “Too” briefly puts her beside the reader as another person looking for possibility.

**“Share” returns with a different object.** The cover says “Stop sharing,” slide 3 says “share / my earnings,” and slide 6 says “share the / choices behind my / work.” The post does not reject openness wholesale; it relocates openness into decisions the reader can use “to communicate / your own ideas.” That move converts a private boundary into a public benefit, answering the implied question of what the audience gets instead.

**The CTA promises to expose the construction of the very thing that persuaded you.** “I’ll show you / how I came up / with the hook / and visual of / this carousel” follows the promise to share choices. The object is already in the reader's hands, so the next post has a concrete subject rather than a vague promise of more tips. “follow along” is earned by the curiosity about how this exact provocation was made.

**This is an Argument expressed through a personal declaration.** “I don’t want,” “I want,” “I understand,” and “I want” carry the sequence from refusal through alternative, concession, and usefulness. There is no literal question chain; each declaration answers an objection opened by the preceding one. The short outbursts “I don’t give a f*ck” and “I look for that too” sit on opposite sides of the tone: one establishes frustration, the other restores generosity before the invitation to follow.
