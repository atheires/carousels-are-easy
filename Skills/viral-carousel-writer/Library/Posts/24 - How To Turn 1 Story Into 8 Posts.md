## Notes

**One experience, eight angles — the same principle as `Angle Multiplication`, proven with a single concrete story.** *"I ran my first marathon"* is deliberately mundane; the point only lands if the source story is ordinary. Choosing a boring example is what makes the claim believable.

**It shows only two of the eight.** Post #4 and post #7. Not #1 and #2 — non-consecutive numbers, which quietly implies the other six exist and are just as developed. **Showing a sample from the middle of a set is stronger evidence of the whole set than showing the beginning of it.**

**Every example is a real post by a named creator**, screenshotted with the account handle attached. @adamwaithe, @pauline.warnier. Not mockups. This is the cheapest credibility available and almost no one uses it — the post proves its method works by pointing at people who already did it.

**The template is three fields: Format, Message, and one line of instruction.** *"Format: Carousel / Message: Lessons I learned / Turn your experience into advice, but this time in carousel format."* Compact enough that a reader can see themselves filling it in for their own story.

**Slide 5 does the widening move before the ask** — *"Every experience you've lived can become content that connects."* One marathon becomes any life. Same placement as [[09 - I Built My Second Brain With 3 Folders|post 09]]'s "this works for everyone" slide: broaden on the penultimate beat so nobody excludes themselves right before the CTA.

**The CTA is the strongest structure seen so far for a *cohort* offer.** *"Comment 'STORY' to join our 21-Day Storytelling Content Challenge."* Note what it does that the weaker CTAs don't:

- The deliverable is **an event with a duration**, not a file — 21 days.
- The verb is **join**, not get. It implies other people.
- The word **STORY** is the subject of the entire post.
- The line above it — *"We'll show you exactly how"* — answers the doubt the post has just created by showing only two of eight.

**Only five slides.** Two examples, a setup, a premise, an ask. It does not need eight slides to prove "eight posts" — a claim in a headline does not have to be enumerated to be trusted, provided the samples shown are real and specific.
