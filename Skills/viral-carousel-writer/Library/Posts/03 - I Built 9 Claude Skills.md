## Notes

**Slides 2–10 are one repeated frame, deliberately.** Blurred still from the cover photo as background, a single blue folder icon, the skill name in plain sentence case, then one or two sentences. Nine slides, identical layout. There is no texture variation at all — the opposite of what the craft file demands — and it works *because the post is an inventory*. The repetition is the point: it reads as a folder you are scrolling through. Texture rules apply to arguments, not to lists.

**Every skill is named as a filename.** `Webinar-Deck`, `VSL-Script`, `Optimize-Ads`. Hyphenated, no spaces, no marketing name. That single choice does more to make the thing feel real and already-built than any claim in the caption.

**Each description answers "what does it do" and then "so what".** Two sentences maximum. The second sentence is always the reason to care — *"very little editing is needed before recording"*, *"the changes are automatically made for me"*, *"trained on over 10K+ direct response emails"*. Never a feature without its consequence.

**Proof is borrowed, specifically and by name.** Russell Brunson, Alex Hormozi, 10K+ direct response emails, 150-200 slides, 1 to 30 minutes. Numbers and names on almost every slide. This is the claim-proof law running at full strength across an entire post.

### The CTA — the part worth stealing

**The ask is on its own slide and nothing else is on it.** No summary, no recap, no second ask. One question, one instruction.

**The escalation is the whole trick:** *"Want Access To ALL These Skills?"* then, in smaller type underneath, *"(Plus 50+ others)"*. The post spent nine slides making nine things wanted, then reveals at the last possible moment that nine was a fraction. The reader's sense of what they are about to get jumps by 6× one line before they are asked to act.

**The grid of blank folders is the visual of the withheld thing.** Not a list, not a description — just identical unlabelled folders. It shows there is more without giving any of it away.

**"Comment 'SKILLS' + I'll send a link"** — the word is lifted from the cover slide, so it has been in the reader's head since the first frame. It is one syllable, impossible to misspell, and it is the literal name of the thing.

**The result: 2.1K comments against 518 likes.** People who would not spend a like *will* spend a comment when a comment is the price of the thing.

**What it gives away and what it holds back:** it names all nine skills and says exactly what each does — so it answers *what* completely. It never says how any of them were built, what is in a skill file, or how to make one. The *how* is entirely behind the word.
