## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
**The 6 *Golden* Exercises**

Not because they're the "best."

Because together they train almost every **major muscle group** your body needs for lifelong strength.

*Anatomical render of a flexed arm, all muscle, no skin. Dr. Longevity — Thomas Paloschi MD. Arrow.*

**2**
**Why 6, not 50 exercises**

Guidelines do not name exercises. They ask for all major muscle groups, twice a week.

Six multi-joint patterns **cover them all.**

*Citation, small print:* Bull et al., WHO Guidelines, Br J Sports Med, 2020. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955

**3–8 — one pattern per slide, identical layout**

Each: a numbered name, a plain-English definition in blue, two bullets (what it loads / what it is the movement behind), then "Options:" and two or three labelled anatomical illustrations.

**1. Squat** — Standing up and sitting down
- Loads **thighs**, **glutes**, trunk
- The movement behind *every chair you get out of*
Options: Lateral lunge · Bodyweight squat · Split squat

**2. Row** — The pull toward you
- Loads **mid-back**, rear shoulders, biceps, grip
- Holds your **posture** up through a day of sitting
Options: Standing cable row · Bent-over barbell row · Seated cable row

**3. Hinge** — Lifting from the floor
- Loads **glutes**, **hamstrings**, **lower back**
- The movement behind *every bag, box and child you pick up*
Options: Dumbbell hinge · Trap-bar deadlift

**4. Push** — Pushing away from you
- Loads **chest**, **shoulders**, **triceps**
- The movement behind *getting up off the floor*
Options: Dumbbell press · Push-Ups

**5. Pull** — Pulling from overhead
- Loads **lats**, **upper back**, **grip**
- The movement behind *reaching up and pulling down*
Options: Pull-up · Lat pull-down

**6. Carry** — Carrying a load and walking
- Loads **grip** and **trunk** while you move
- The movement *behind shopping, luggage, a child on one hip*
Options: Suitcase carry · Loaded march

## Notes

**The hook works by pre-empting the objection in the subhead.** *"Not because they're the 'best.'"* The scare quotes around *best* kill the argument the fitness audience always starts. Then the actual reason lands: *because together they cover everything.* **Answering the objection on slide 1 rather than slide 2** — worth contrasting with post 04, which saved each objection for the following slide. Both work; the difference is that here the objection would have stopped the swipe.

**Slide 2 exists only to justify the number.** *"Why 6, not 50 exercises"* — it defends the constraint before teaching anything. The number in the headline is the promise (Easy: only six things), so the number is what has to survive scrutiny first.

**Every exercise gets the same second bullet, and it is the best writing in the post:** *"The movement behind every chair you get out of." "Every bag, box and child you pick up." "Shopping, luggage, a child on one hip."* The abstract pattern is translated into a domestic moment the reader had this week. **Loads → the anatomy. The movement behind → the life.** Muscle names alone would be a textbook; the second bullet is what makes it a reason to care.

**One real citation, in tiny print, once.** Bull et al., WHO Guidelines, with a DOI. It is not shouted and it is not repeated. Its presence on the one slide that makes the load-bearing claim is enough to colour the whole carousel as sourced.

**No CTA at all.** No comment word, no "follow for more", no offer. The last slide is just the sixth exercise. The only persistent element is the MD credential in the footer of every slide. This is the counter-example to posts 03–09: a post that harvests authority and saves rather than leads. Worth noting because it is a deliberate choice, not an omission — the account is a doctor's brand where the credibility *is* the funnel.

**The illustration style is the credibility.** Clinical greyscale anatomy with the working muscles highlighted in red, on white. It looks like a medical reference, not a fitness post, which is the same principle as post 08: **the visual register is an argument about who is talking.**
