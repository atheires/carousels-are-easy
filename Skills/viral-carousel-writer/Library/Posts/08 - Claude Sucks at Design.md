## Notes

**The hook attacks the thing the audience loves.** *"CLAUDE SUCKS AT DESIGN."* Full stop, largest type on the slide. Every other post in this file opens with a promise; this one opens with an insult aimed at the tool the reader uses daily. It forces a reaction — agree and you need the fix, disagree and you have to swipe to argue.

**The resolution is one small line at the bottom: "These 42 skills fix it."** Tiny type inside a thin outlined box, dwarfed by the headline. The problem is shouted, the solution is whispered. That imbalance is what makes it a hook rather than an ad — you have to lean in for the second half.

**The design does what the post claims.** A carousel arguing that Claude produces generic design cannot itself look generic. It is styled as a 1970s technical spec sheet — registration marks, dimension lines, `SYSTEM.STATUS: ONLINE`, `BUILD: 2025.05.24`, `PRECISION: 8-BIT CLEAN`. **The format is the proof of the claim.** Compare the folder-icon slides in post 03: functional, forgettable, and fine there because that post's claim was about capability, not taste. Here the claim is about taste, so the artefact has to demonstrate it.

**42 items across 5 slides, and it never feels like a wall.** Because each slide is a *named layer* with its own count in the headline — 10 core, 8 creative, 10 system, 8 prompt, 6 security. The reader is never asked to hold 42 things, only "six layers". Numbering runs continuously 01–42 across slides, so the progress bar is built into the content.

**Every item is `slug-name` + a three-to-four-word gloss.** Never a sentence. `token-budgets: Context window token math`. The parallelism is absolute, which is what lets the eye scan forty-two rows without fatigue. Same filename-style naming as post 03 — hyphenated lowercase reads as *a real thing that exists on a disk*.

**Each slide ends on a bolded one-line summary** — *"Engineering flawless design output." / "Visual-first programmatic assets." / "Designing systems that respond."* A footer that tells you what the layer was *for*, after showing you what is in it. It converts a list into an argument.

**The CTA renders the Instagram comment box itself, as a technical drawing.** It shows the reader the exact UI element they are about to use, dimensioned like a machined part. The instruction is not described, it is *depicted*. Nothing else is on the slide.

**The caption gives away the method the post withheld.** The slides list the 42 skills; the caption explains *how to combine them* ("Don't install all 42 at once. Stack 3-4 that fight slop at the source, then add eyes"). Value in the caption, artefact behind the comment word — the reader gets something real for free and still has a reason to comment.

**15,936 comments on 9,542 likes.** The pattern across posts 03, 05, 06, 07 and 08 is now unambiguous: **a named, countable artefact plus a one-word ask outperforms every other CTA shape**, and the count in the headline ("9", "4", "42") is doing much of the work.
