# 51 — How to Deal With a Bad Mental Health Day

**Account:** @bradandthegoat  
**Slides:** 11

## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
**how to deal with a bad mental health day**

advice from a goose

**2**
**wake up feeling awful?**

fine. we ride at dawn.

**3**
**lower the expectations.**

today’s goal  
is simply: do not completely lose the plot.

**4**
**drink some water.**

yes, actual water. not just caffeine and rage.

**5**
**have a tactical lie down.**

not giving up. just rebooting the goose.

**6**
**brain says you’re useless?**

tell it to be quiet. it has no qualifications.

**7**
**someone annoys you?**

do not reply immediately.  
go hiss at a fence first.

**8**
**eat something.**

you cannot fight imaginary  
disasters on an empty stomach.

**9**
**cancel unnecessary nonsense.**

protect the pond. protect the peace.

**10**
**if today is awful,**

go to bed early out of pure spite.

**11**
**tomorrow we try again.**

until then: HONK. HISS. SURVIVE.

## Notes

**The cover sells relief through a mismatch.** *“how to deal with a bad mental health day”* is a familiar self-help promise, then *“advice from a goose”* lowers the stakes and signals comic permission. The reader expects practical care but also understands the voice will be silly, which makes the carousel inviting on a hard day.

**Slide 2 joins the reader at their lowest-energy moment.** *“wake up feeling awful?”* names a recognizable start, and *“fine. we ride at dawn.”* answers with mock bravado instead of demanding optimism. It acknowledges the bad feeling before the list starts, so the humor reads as companionship rather than dismissal.

**Slide 3 changes the goal from achievement to damage control.** *“lower the expectations.”* is followed by *“today’s goal is simply: do not completely lose the plot.”* The word *“simply”* makes the modest target sound intentional; this is the first useful reframing after the opening joke, and it gives the reader a standard they can actually meet.

**Slides 4–5 keep the advice bodily and make it funny in the same breath.** *“drink some water”* gets the qualifier *“not just caffeine and rage,”* then *“have a tactical lie down”* becomes *“rebooting the goose.”* Each command is ordinary self-maintenance; the goose’s imagined military vocabulary turns it into a memorable bit without obscuring the action.

**Slides 6–7 move from self-talk to social friction.** *“brain says you’re useless?”* is met with *“it has no qualifications,”* then *“someone annoys you?”* leads to *“go hiss at a fence first.”* The sequence shifts from an internal critic to an irritating person, but both replies create a pause before the reader believes a harsh thought or sends a regrettable response.

**Slides 8–9 widen care into basic needs and boundaries.** *“you cannot fight imaginary disasters on an empty stomach”* gives a reason for eating that fits the anxious mood, while *“protect the pond. protect the peace.”* turns cancelling low-value obligations into the goose’s territory-protection joke. The progression is practical: fuel yourself, then remove avoidable demands.

**The ending makes rest feel like a temporary truce.** *“if today is awful, go to bed early out of pure spite”* converts quitting the day into comic defiance; *“tomorrow we try again”* then offers a small, believable reset. *“HONK. HISS. SURVIVE.”* closes with the established goose voice, not a sudden inspirational slogan, so the reassurance feels earned.

**This is an Inventory with a comic persona, not a question-chain argument.** The slides offer discrete responses to common bad-day moments, and the question marks on slides 2, 6, and 7 introduce situations rather than building a logical interrogation. The real progression runs from accepting a rough start to lowering demands, tending to the body, handling thoughts and people, protecting energy, and restarting tomorrow.
