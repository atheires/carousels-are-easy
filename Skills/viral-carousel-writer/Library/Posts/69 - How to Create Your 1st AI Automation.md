## Notes

**The cover sells a clear beginner outcome: “How to Create Your 1st AI Automation.”** “1st” makes the promise feel attainable, while “AI Automation” names the result directly. The next slide delays the how-to steps by first giving the reader a framework for choosing a task.

**“Know Your RAOS” is a planning gate before any tool instruction.** The four questions unpack the acronym into repeated work, actions, output, and system. That sequence forces the reader to define the process and destination before automating it, so the tutorial starts with a use case rather than a product feature.

**Slide 3 makes the framework concrete with “My RAOS Example.”** Each abstract prompt receives a specific answer, ending in “One central place where everything is organized automatically.” The bottom line—“Now I know exactly what I want to automate”—states the decision the example was designed to produce and hands off cleanly to setup.

**The steps then move from setup to specification: “Open Claude App in your Desktop,” followed by “Turn Your RAOS Into a Prompt.”** The first is deliberately brief, while the next supplies the key thinking work: “The clearer the prompt, the better the automation.” The unusually detailed sample prompt demonstrates what clarity means instead of leaving the reader with a vague instruction to be specific.

**“Let Claude Build It” lowers the perceived technical burden but sets a boundary.** The promise that Claude can handle searching, filtering, summarizing, and saving is balanced by the next slide’s connector setup. The phrase “You don’t need to manually build every step” keeps the reader moving while the visible app sequence shows that a working automation still needs choices and connections.

**The tutorial builds in a test before it claims completion: “Run it once before making it automatic.”** The three checks—usefulness, reliable sources, and missing results—give the reader concrete quality criteria. The sample output makes this step legible by showing both a populated “AI Opportunities” table and an explicit limitation: “I’m only missing a few from X (Twitter).”

**The CTA follows the demonstration with “If you want the complete ai automation claude guide.”** The guide is positioned as an extension of the process just shown, and “Comment ‘Automation’” gives one simple response action. The offer comes after the reader has received the framework, worked example, prompt, build guidance, integrations, and test; it feels like a continuation of the tutorial. The genre is Build: a scaffolded sequence takes a beginner from task selection to a tested first workflow.
