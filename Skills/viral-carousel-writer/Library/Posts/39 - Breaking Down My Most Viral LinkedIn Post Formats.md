An Indian software engineer got an international job offer

helped.

But it’s also how he approached job search, the way you  
apply matters more than how many places you apply to  
most people are just applying and hoping something  
works.

## Notes

**The cover promises an explanation of owned results.** “Breaking down my / MOST VIRAL / LinkedIn Post Formats” makes “my” the authority claim: these are formats attached to posts the writer actually published. “Swipe to / know / how I / did it” turns the visible impression counts into a question about cause. The reader is being offered access to decisions behind outcomes, rather than another unsupported list of viral hooks.

**Slide 2 starts with the format most dependent on externally grounded substance.** “Research, don’t just ask AI” precedes the hook formula and the ₹1.8 crore example. That instruction matters because the cover has primed the reader to chase numbers. The slide redirects them toward “a real case study/story” and “exactly how the / result happened” before showing how to package the result. The supplied first teaching slide is labelled “FORMAT 2,” and the next is “FORMAT 1”; their order is retained.

**The case-study hook makes the obstacle sharpen the number.** “worth ₹1.8 crore, without any / overseas network” is followed by “Here’s how he did it →.” The salary alone is impressive; “without” creates the apparent contradiction that needs explaining. The embedded post then answers with visa-sponsoring companies, job descriptions, preparation, and resume changes. Its “Of course, his experience ... / helped” also qualifies the success story rather than pretending the mechanism was effortless.

**The move from case study to autobiography changes the proof without abandoning causality.** “2021: My first client paid me ₹15,000 a month. / 2026: We’re a 7-figure agency” is followed by “one key learning / that got you there.” The years give a before and after, but “because of this one / LEARNING” insists on a bridge. In the embedded example, “An editor went missing. A client changed the brief” makes “You need systems, not just people” answer a concrete failure rather than float as entrepreneurial wisdom.

**The “BORING” label gives the reader permission to use an ordinary thought.** “the BORING / RELATABLE quote” follows two forms built around impressive results. “My 2 AM thoughts on turning 25...” then supplies friends studying, quitting jobs, marrying, and travelling before “It’s never about timelines.” Those examples make the conclusion answer a lived comparison. The instruction to check connection demographics explains why the unexceptional subject could matter intensely to this particular audience.

**The rant changes recognition into dialogue, then leaves the institution without an answer.** “Person 1: [What they usually say] / Person 2: [What everyone is / actually thinking]” follows the quieter relatable reflection. In the sample, “Could you share your last 3 months’ pay slips?” earns “Why?”; each procedural justification earns a sharper objection until “HR: That’s how hiring works / Candidate:” ends in silence. The question chain exposes the power imbalance through replies the reader wishes someone would say.

**The main lesson is repeatedly to explain what follows the hook.** “Start with the final impact + / numbers, then explain how it / happened,” “And then explain it more...,” and “Then add some examples / + ONE relatable takeaway” recur across very different forms. The last form makes this especially clear: “That ATS is rejecting your resume. / Wrong!” is followed by an explanation of what the system does and steps for the applicant. “Wrong!” earns attention, but the explanation and usable resource supply the value that the provocation alone cannot.

**This is Curation of the writer's own examples, ending by extending the final giveaway form.** “step by step GUIDE / + a GIVEAWAY” immediately precedes “comment / LINKEDIN” for “AI prompts for each / format.” The reader has seen the categories, formulas, and real applications before being offered help using them. There is a productive tension with “don’t just ask AI”: the initial demand is for researched material, while the final offer makes AI the writing aid. “gyaan hehehe :)” brings the ending back to an informal person after several slides of instruction and performance evidence.
