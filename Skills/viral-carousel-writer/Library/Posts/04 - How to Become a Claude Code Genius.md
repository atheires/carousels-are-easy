## Notes

**The cover makes an abstract thing physical.** A phrenology head with the feature names written into the skull regions. It communicates "there are eight parts to this and they fit together" before a single word of explanation. Compare with post 03, where the same account used a photo of himself — the choice tracks the content: an inventory gets a person, a system gets a diagram.

**Slide 3 is the credibility slide and it is placed third, not first.** Two slides of "this thing is big" come before any claim about himself. The Stripe list is the proof and the sticky note is the interpretation — the numbers are shown, then translated into one sentence a reader can repeat: *"clients paying me $5k+ per project to build systems for them."* Raw evidence plus a plain-English gloss.

**Every teaching slide ends on a dangling objection.** Slide 4 closes *"But it doesn't have any type of personality, or any special abilities…"* → slide 5 answers it with CLAUDE.md. Slide 5 closes *"But so far this isn't any different from a system prompt…"* → slide 6 answers it with Skills. This is the question chain running explicitly: **each slide voices the reader's next doubt in the reader's own words, then the next slide resolves it.** The ellipsis is doing the work every time.

**It escalates the stakes each time it answers.** Basics → "no personality" → CLAUDE.md → "that's just a system prompt" → Skills → *"Skills are the real game-changer."* The objection is never dismissed; it is conceded and then out-bid.

**Handwritten annotation is used as a second voice.** The typed copy explains; the scrawled notes ("here's one of mine", "it looks like this") point. It reads like someone talking over their own screen share, and it costs nothing in reading time.

**It breaks the hide-every-how rule on purpose, and then re-gates.** Slides 4–6 genuinely teach — you could go and make a skill folder from slide 6 alone. What is withheld is not the mechanism but the *completeness*: the head on slide 1 has eight labelled regions and the post covers three. The CTA sells the missing five. **The gap the cover opened is the gap the CTA closes.**

**"CLAUDE" as the comment word** — the single most obvious word in the post, impossible to get wrong, and already in the reader's head from slide 1.
