# 22 — Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable

**URL:** https://www.instagram.com/p/DcB6rvxCb4j/
**Slides:** 10
**Type:** Argument, at the highest register in the file. Set as a printed essay — letterpress serif on off-white paper stock, hand-drawn diagrams, no photography, no colour.

---

## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
`BRANDING FRAMEWORK`

**Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable**

`WHY WE LOVE THE BRANDS WE LOVE`

**2**
Nobody loves a whole brand. People become obsessed with three to five touchpoints inside it, and the rest they never think about again.

*Which raises the question this post is about*

**What makes one touchpoint capable of that** — **when the twenty next to it are not?**

**3**
**Everything in your business is decent. *That is the problem.***

Decent is what function buys you, and it is the ceiling of doing the job correctly. No amount of doing the job more correctly moves you past it.

*Diagram: six identical empty squares labelled* `DECENT — SAME MATERIAL, REPEATED` *→ a single crosshatched diamond labelled* `EXTRAORDINARY`

The work is going from decent to extraordinary, and extraordinary is not a better version of decent.

**4**
**The reason is *how memory works***

Memory does not record what it predicted. If the package arrives when it was supposed to, nothing happened. The customer is not unimpressed. They are unaware.

*Diagram: four circles with dots draining away —* `ON TIME` → `ON TIME` → `ON TIME` → `NOTHING RECORDED`

This is why a company can execute flawlessly for years and remain completely invisible.

**9**
**Doing it right is not a strategy. It is the entry fee.**

**01** Function is invisible. Meeting the expectation leaves no memory at all.
**02** What gets loved sits above function, in the part that was never necessary.
**03** The unnecessary is the only proof that somebody decided, and the decision is what people attach to.
**04** No competitor could have made the same one. Copyable belongs to the category, not to you.
**05** And it has to happen every single time. Once is an anecdote. Every time is a belief.

Miss one and you have a touchpoint that works, which has never made anybody obsessed with anything.

**10 — CTA**
Knowing the conditions is not the same as knowing how many touchpoints you need, where they have to live, or which one to build first.

**Comment *LOVED* to get it first.**

## Notes

**The hook is a paradox stated flatly.** *"Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable."* No number, no audience label, no promise of a method. It works because it accuses the reader of a virtue — the thing they are proudest of is the thing being blamed. Note the eyebrow (`BRANDING FRAMEWORK`) and the footer (`WHY WE LOVE THE BRANDS WE LOVE`) do the audience-targeting that the headline refuses to do, so the headline stays clean.

**Slide 2 states the question the post exists to answer, and says so.** *"Which raises the question this post is about."* Explicit, unembarrassed. Then the question itself is genuinely hard: *"What makes one touchpoint capable of that when the twenty next to it are not?"* **Naming the question on slide 2 sets the contract** — everything after is measured against whether it answers this.

**"Everything in your business is decent. That is the problem."** Two sentences, the second reversing the first. This is the same construction as [[10 - The 6 Golden Exercises|post 10]]'s *"Not because they're the 'best.'"* — take the reader's assumed compliment and make it the diagnosis.

**"Extraordinary is not a better version of decent."** The line the whole post turns on, and it is a *category* claim rather than a *degree* claim. The diagram makes it visual before the sentence lands: six identical squares, then one diamond. Not a bigger square.

**Slide 4 supplies the mechanism, and this is what separates it from an opinion post.** *"Memory does not record what it predicted."* Then the consequence in three escalating clauses: *"nothing happened. The customer is not unimpressed. They are unaware."* The middle clause pre-empts the obvious misreading and the third replaces it with something worse. **Correcting the reader's likely paraphrase mid-sentence is a very high-level move.**

**"They are unaware"** is the whole insight in three words — worse than disliked, because there is nothing to fix.

**The diagrams are drawn, not decorative.** Dots draining out of circles across four "ON TIME" deliveries into `NOTHING RECORDED`. It shows *accumulating nothing*, which is a hard idea to write and an easy one to draw. Compare [[08 - Claude Sucks at Design|post 08]] and [[04 - How to Become a Claude Code Genius|post 04]] — the recurring pattern is that **the abstract claim gets a picture, the concrete claim gets a screenshot.**

**Slide 9 is a five-condition list that is genuinely a *set*, not a listicle.** *"Miss one and you have a touchpoint that works, which has never made anybody obsessed with anything."* The closing line tells you the five are jointly necessary — so the reader cannot cherry-pick, which is exactly what makes the CTA necessary.

**"Once is an anecdote. Every time is a belief."** Five words each side. The best-compressed line in the file.

**The CTA is the most sophisticated one here.** It does not say "want the rest?". It draws a precise line between what has been delivered and what has not:

> *"Knowing the conditions is not the same as knowing how many touchpoints you need, where they have to live, or which one to build first."*

The post gave the *why* completely and withheld the *how* completely — and then **named the three specific unanswered questions** so the reader feels the shape of the gap. This is the vault's own "answer every why, hide every how" executed better than the vault currently describes it.

**"Comment *LOVED* to get it first."** The word is lifted from the post's own vocabulary (*"what gets loved"*, *"why we love the brands we love"*), and *"first"* adds urgency without a deadline.
