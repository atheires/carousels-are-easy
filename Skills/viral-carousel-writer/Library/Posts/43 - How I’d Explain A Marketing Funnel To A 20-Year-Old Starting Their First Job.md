## Notes

**The hook attacks the difficulty of the explanation before it attacks the textbook.** “How I’d explain a marketing funnel to a 20-year-old starting their first job.” A new employee is a precise test of whether the writer can make the concept usable. “How I’d explain” promises a human voice rather than a definition; “first job” makes ignorance reasonable. The later “Forget everything about: Awareness. Consideration. Conversion.” fulfils that promise by suspending the vocabulary that makes the subject feel harder than buying shoes.

**The supplied slide 2 is the cover, so its next numbered image gives the conclusion unusually early.** “The real job of marketing is to answer a different question at each stage.” This follows the headline in the supplied order, not an introductory scene. It immediately replaces a diagram with a job the reader can understand. The files place “For a minute.” much later; that phrase reads as an opening qualification, but the evidence does not establish its original position. The useful distinction here is between what the copy implies and what the numbered source actually supplies.

**The sneaker example makes the cover’s age and employment detail matter.** “Let’s say you just got your first salary.” → “Budget: $100.” The salary gives the purchase a motive and the budget gives the later hesitation stakes. “I’m now entering the awareness stage of Nike’s funnel.” is deliberately absurd internal dialogue. “Obviously.” then lets the novice laugh with the writer at the terminology rather than feel laughed at for not knowing it.

**The question chain changes the buyer’s task, not merely the name of the stage.** “Why should I notice you?” → “Why should I care?” → “Why should I trust you?” → “Why should I choose you?” → “Why should I come back?” Attention must happen before interest; interest still leaves a credibility problem; credibility still leaves alternatives. Ending with “come back” extends the explanation beyond the visible sale. The repeated “Why should I” makes every stage answerable from the customer’s side, which is the promised change in perspective.

**Desire is followed by resistance because wanting the shoes creates something to lose.** “Maybe I want this.” is tentative, not a purchase commitment. “You start looking for reasons not to buy.” then names the apparently backward move. “Is $100 worth it?” uses the exact budget already introduced, before the questions widen into alternatives, reviews, durability and cheaper sellers. Those are recognisable objections, so the reader experiences consideration before needing its label.

**The main insight separates a model of decisions from a prediction of footsteps.** “Customers are messy. The funnel is just a way to make sense of the mess.” answers the textbook’s “Nice. Clean.” with forgetting, reminders and detours. The sequence includes “Forget about it” and “Forget again,” admitting inactivity between contacts. “A sale is rarely one big decision.” then states the durable insight: the model explains accumulated decisions even when a person does not move neatly through channels.

**The short lines make tiny changes of mind audible.** “Could be an ad. / Could be a creator. / Could be your friend.” keeps the discovery channel variable while the psychological event stays constant. “I’ve seen this.” becomes “Maybe I want this.” and finally “You buy.” The cautious “Maybe” is doing work: it prevents recognition from being mistaken for intent. The last blunt verb only arrives once reassurance, a discount, scarcity or a friend’s recommendation can resolve the remaining hesitation.

**The CTA sells the translation the post has already demonstrated.** “What your customer is thinking at each stage. / What your marketing should do there.” turns the customer’s internal questions into a practical cheat sheet. “Comment ‘FUNNEL’” is easy to understand because the term now means smaller decisions rather than a jargon diagram. This is an **Argument taught through a purchase example**: “And not really how most people behave.” challenges the tidy account, while the sneaker scenes supply the alternative. The supplied CTA appears at 10, so it cannot honestly be described as the final numbered slide.
