`8 DETAILS YOUR BRAIN NOTICES`

**2 — Uncanny *Recognition***

Show someone an AI image. Don't tell them it's AI.

Watch their eyes. There's always a micro-pause. "Wait, something's…"

They can't name it. But their brain flags it anyway.

That's the uncanny valley now. Not obvious glitches. Not melted hands. Subtle wrongness in details that most people don't consciously process.

**Realism lives in those invisible signals.**

**3 — Skin *Texture***
Perfect porcelain skin screams "render." Real skin shows:

`Visible pores (not uniform, clustered differently)` · `Oil variation (T-zone sheen, matte cheeks)` · `Uneven tone (slight redness, color shifts)` · `Micro texture (not airbrushed smooth)`

**If it looks magazine-retouched perfect — that's your tell. Real faces are messy up close.**

**4 — Lighting *Behaviour***
"Good lighting" and "realistic lighting" aren't the same. Natural light behaves specifically:

`Soft falloff (gradual, not hard cutoff)` · `Shadow depth variation (some hard, some soft)` · `Bounce light (color cast from nearby surfaces)` · `Uneven reflections (not mirror-perfect)`

**Flat even lighting? That's 3D render logic. Not photography.**

**9 — Biggest gap. And *hardest to fake*.**

Real visuals transmit feeling:

- Calm → soft light, pastel tones, breathing space
- Tension → high contrast, tight crop, hard shadows
- Intimacy → warm tones, shallow depth, close framing
- Luxury → dramatic light, rich texture, precise composition

AI generates "neutral pleasant." That reads as nothing. Empty.

**If an image doesn't make you feel something — it's wallpaper, not communication.**

**10**
**Most people stop at "does this look realistic?"**

Wrong question.

Better question: **"Would my brain accept this as a photograph someone took?"**

That requires:
- Light behaving like physics
- Texture showing like materials
- Depth reading like optics
- Emotion landing like intent

The gap between "AI image" and "brand visual" is that checklist.

## Notes

**The cover image is itself the argument.** A moody, imperfect, heavily-shadowed photograph — the opposite of "neutral pleasant" AI output. Third instance in this file of a post whose artefact demonstrates its own claim (see 08, 12).

**Slide 2 describes a physical reaction rather than stating a fact.** *"Watch their eyes. There's always a micro-pause. 'Wait, something's…'"* The reader is asked to recall an experience they have had, so the claim is verified from their own memory before any evidence is offered. **Describing the reader's involuntary reaction is stronger than asserting the thing that causes it.**

**Every detail slide ends on a diagnostic sentence the reader can apply immediately** — *"If it looks magazine-retouched perfect — that's your tell."* / *"Flat even lighting? That's 3D render logic. Not photography."* Each is a test, not a description.

**The four-chip layout does a lot of work.** Four short parenthetical specifics laid over the image as small dark chips — *"Oil variation (T-zone sheen, matte cheeks)"*. The parenthesis carries the precision, the phrase carries the meaning. It lets a slide hold four technical points without becoming a wall.

**Slide 9 is the emotional-register table and it is the best content in the post.** Four states, each mapped to three concrete visual choices. *"Calm → soft light, pastel tones, breathing space."* Then the kill line: *"AI generates 'neutral pleasant.' That reads as nothing. Empty."*

**"If an image doesn't make you feel something — it's wallpaper, not communication."** Generalises straight to copy. A slide that transmits no feeling is wallpaper too.

**Slide 10 is a question-swap, and it is the move worth stealing.** *"Most people stop at 'does this look realistic?' Wrong question. Better question: 'Would my brain accept this as a photograph someone took?'"* It reframes the reader's whole standard in three lines, then makes the four preceding slides into the criteria for the new question. **Replacing the reader's question is a bigger gift than answering it.**

**The CTA is a service enquiry, not a lead magnet.** *"Want help making AI visuals feel real? Comment 🔍 below."* Better than posts 14/15/17 from the same account — there is an offer behind it — but still weaker than posts 03/08/09, because no artefact is named.
