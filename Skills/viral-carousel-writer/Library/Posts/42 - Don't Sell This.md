## Notes

**The supplied cover makes a prohibition useful before the title explains the scope.** “DON'T SELL THIS...” names “Wall Art,” then “SELL THIS INSTEAD...” names “Interactive Dashboards.” The attack is on a specific product choice, not the reader's ambition to sell digital products. Slide 2 then supplies “3 DIGITAL PRODUCTS / I'D NEVER SELL / IN 2026” and “& what to sell instead,” giving the reader a count and a personal judgment after the first example. That is the provided sequence, even though the title reads like a conventional cover.

**The numbers give refusal an apparent market reason before the alternative supplies a buyer reason.** “1.5M people searching it and 12M listings / already selling it” leads to “Opportunity score: 22/100.” The dashboard is then justified through daily use and “an ongoing problem.” The argument changes from how crowded the old market appears to why someone might keep using the new product. The scores and counts are claims in the frame; the writing provides no visible source or calculation for them.

**The parenthesis quietly carries the old product into the new one.** “(hang / it on the wall if they want)” appears inside the description of dashboards after rejecting wall art. It gives the reader a bridge: choosing utility need not surrender every use associated with decoration. “every / single day” then stresses recurrence, which prepares “higher ticket / is an easy yes” to sound like a consequence of lasting value rather than simply charging more.

**The first ideas slide answers the practical objection that the replacement is too abstract.** “Interactive Dashboards” is followed by “Family command center,” “Debt payoff dashboard,” “Perimenopause tracker,” and “Postpartum recovery board.” The list moves across household, financial, and bodily needs rather than repeating one planner with different names. After the broad commercial claim, these nouns let readers recognise a particular person and recurring problem they could serve.

**The central line changes what the seller believes the product is.** “You're selling back business owners time, not / a pretty file” comes after the numerical rejection of digital stickers. It replaces a description of the thing made with a description of what the buyer regains. “Niche it” then follows naturally: if the value is saved time, “Wedding photographer” and “Therapist post kit” should solve different work. This is the post's clearest statement of its underlying commercial insight.

**The children’s-product comparison makes convenience concrete enough to hear.** “No printer, no ink, no cutting” follows the rejection of coloring books. The three repeated negatives name chores a parent would otherwise have to do before a child could use the purchase. “parents say / yes faster when they need something quick” connects that removal of work to the buying moment, and “holds a kid's attention” adds the use-value after the ease of starting.

**The implied question chain alternates a verdict with permission to act.** “DON'T SELL THIS...” / “SELL THIS INSTEAD...” is followed by “DASHBOARD IDEAS,” “TEMPLATE KIT IDEAS,” and “KIDS GAME IDEAS.” Each pair answers why the old choice looks weak and what a replacement could be; each list then answers what the reader could actually make. The repeated headers make those shifts easy to follow, while “Severely / oversaturated” becomes the shorter “Oversaturated” for coloring books, preserving a distinction rather than calling every category equally bad.

**This is an Argument / Inventory hybrid whose CTA sells implementation after supplying choices.** “WANT TO LEARN HOW TO / CREATE THESE DIGITAL / PRODUCTS IN MINUTES?” follows three alternatives and their concrete idea lists. “THESE” points back to products the reader can now imagine, so the offer resolves the next barrier: making them. “115+ prompts,” customization guides, and lessons on “niching, & / selling” extend the earlier product and buyer reasoning. “Comment LIBRARY” is earned by the abundance of examples, though “IN MINUTES” introduces a speed promise the supplied frames do not themselves demonstrate.
