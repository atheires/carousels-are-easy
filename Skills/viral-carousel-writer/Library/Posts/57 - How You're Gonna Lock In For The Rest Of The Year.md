## Notes

**The cover addresses a reader who wants a recovery, not merely a routine.** "How you’re gonna lock in for the rest of the year" supplies a remaining window of time, while "and pull that comeback" assumes a disappointing stretch came before it. "Gonna" makes the promise sound like a friend's confident instruction. "No gatekeeping" and "my full guide" then promise access to the practical means, giving the ambition somewhere to go.

**Slide 2 deliberately shrinks the scale of the promised comeback.** "Most people quit because they set the bar way too high on day one" follows a cover loaded with urgency. It explains why answering that urgency with a huge effort would fail. "One commit or ten pages a day" makes the correction tangible; "And never miss twice" introduces a recovery rule so a single bad day cannot disqualify the reader from the comeback.

**The goal slide replaces a finish-line question with an identity question.** "Forget the goal" follows "Start small," pushing beyond merely reducing the size of a target. "Who you want to be in 2027" gives the small actions a direction without demanding a result before the year ends. "So the new year isn't another fresh start" is the sting: the reader's appealing annual reset is recast as evidence that the previous attempt never became a way of living.

**The phone slide supplies the missing time after supplying the intended person.** "Your phone is where the day goes" comes after deciding who to become. The sentence treats wasted time as a place the reader already knows, not a moral failure. "Once scrolling takes effort" identifies the intervention: make the competing habit harder. "You can put it into the habits from the last slide" closes the causal loop between attention recovered and the identity just chosen.

**The physical advice lowers the author's authority just as it broadens the demands.** "I'm lacking here myself, so this one's for me as well" appears when the post adds exercise, food, and sleep to the reader's obligations. "Just look at my numbers lol" repeats that admission rather than pretending a perfect routine has been achieved. The cycle sentence then gives the item a reason to belong: changing one behaviour affects the others, so the body is part of the proposed comeback rather than a separate fitness topic.

**The reflection slide returns to the opening rule against doing too much.** "Pick one small thing to do better next week" follows the cycle of sleep, training, and eating, which might otherwise suggest fixing all three immediately. "Don't try to change everything at once" repeats the concern about the high bar on day one at a later point where overload is now more plausible. The main insight is cumulative: an identity changes through small repeated actions, then through small corrections to those actions.

**The tool claims work because personal language keeps interrupting the resource lists.** "My go-to blocker is Opal, but anything works" specifies what the author uses and immediately removes dependence on that brand. "I do mine in Notion and Claude" later makes the software part of an existing Sunday habit. The closing "I haven't organized it manually in months" gives automation a particular job: reduce the upkeep of reflection, so tracking does not become another reason to quit.

**The guide CTA has been seeded through conspicuously unfinished lists.** "+5 more suggestions!" in slide 2 and "+5 more free ones" in slide 3 promise additional material while giving useful examples on the slide itself. After habits, resources, a blocker, training tools, and a reflection method, "Comment ‘GUIDE’" refers back to the cover's "my full guide." The reader has evidence of what that guide will contain, although the final frame leaves the delivery mechanism unstated.

**This is a Build with resource curation inside it.** "Start small" → "Forget the goal" → "Put the phone down" → "Train your body" → "Reflect Weekly" assembles a way to sustain the comeback, rather than five independent productivity tricks. There is no literal sequence of questions; the implied questions move from how to begin, to what to aim at, to where the time comes from, to what supports the work, to how to keep improving. The books and apps answer those questions alongside the instructions.
