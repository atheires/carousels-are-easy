## Notes

**The cover hijacks the promise of an easy improvement guide.** “how to waste your marketing budget / in 6 easy steps” puts a desirable instructional shape around an undesirable result. “easy” suggests these errors require little effort and may already be happening. The reader is invited to recognise waste, not merely consume another list of ways to optimise spending.

**Slide 2 punctures apparent abundance with a tiny actual change.** “make 30 versions of the same ad” initially sounds like energetic testing; “change three words in the hook” shows how little changed. That numerical mismatch does the accusation more precisely than “your ads lack variety.” “Meta is gonna LOVE all these exciting choices!!!” then overperforms enthusiasm so the reader hears the author's disbelief in the label “creative diversification.”

**The next mistake moves from the advertiser's activity to the customer's interrupted intention.** “send every click to the homepage” follows an ad-production error with the destination of the traffic it produces. “one specific product/offer” establishes a clear reason for clicking before “start their journey over from the beginning.” The sarcastic “naturally” asks the reader to hear how unnatural the instruction is; the wink keeps the criticism conversational without undoing it.

**The first question pair imitates the speed of an anxious operator.** “campaign had a weird morning? REBUILD IT” → “new ad hasn't converted after eleven minutes? KILL IT ASAP!” makes an observation instantly become a drastic command. “weird morning” and the absurdly exact “eleven minutes” expose the thin evidence. After the homepage problem, this slide shifts to the advertiser's handling of results, showing another way effort can destroy rather than improve a campaign.

**The vague-copy example lets the reader discover the missing product.** “‘elevate your everyday.’ beautiful. what are we selling? who knows <3” follows the loud intervention slide with a soft, polished line that says almost nothing. “beautiful” grants the aesthetic appeal for one beat before the blunt commercial question removes it. The affection symbol makes the failure sound charmingly indefensible, which is the joke: pleasant language has concealed the absence of an offer.

**The discount question chain ends in the consequence hidden inside the supposed fix.** “sales are down? 20% OFF!!! still down? 30% OFF!!!” repeats the intervention rhythm of slide 4, now escalating the size of the concession. “eventually you can run a GOING OUT OF BIZ sale” is the darkest payoff: the seller's last sale becomes the destination of discounting as the automatic answer. The insight lands inside the example rather than in a separate warning about margins.

**The final practice names the surrender of judgment behind the other fixes.** “they MUST know something you don’t” makes assumed competitor authority explicit. “no need to investigate whether it’s actually working for them...” then leaves the omitted investigation hanging. After tiny ad variants, restless changes and automatic discounts, this last item broadens the problem from bad tactics to decisions made without evidence; it is a fitting endpoint even though the six items are not a strict sequence of prerequisites.

**The CTA finally gives a sincere instruction, then makes the inversion explicit.** “follow us for daily marketing and ecomm advice :)” is the first plainly useful imperative after six destructive ones. “(just don’t do any of these things)” resolves the joke and prevents the advice voice from remaining ambiguous. The follow is earned by the specificity of the diagnoses, rather than by a promised resource the slides have not demonstrated.

**This is a satirical Inventory, with a small argument inside each item.** “make your ads incredibly vague” is deliberately bad advice; “what are we selling?” supplies the test that defeats it. There is no single late revelation and no master question chain across all six mistakes. Each heading is prosecuted by its own miniature scene, with “LOVE,” “REBUILD,” “KILL” and “OFF” letting the reader hear the overconfidence that causes the waste.
