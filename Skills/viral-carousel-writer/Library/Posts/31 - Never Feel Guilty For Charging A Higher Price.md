## Notes

**It argues with a feeling, not a strategy.** The cover is not "how to raise your prices" — it is *"never feel guilty."* The obstacle being removed is shame. This is the belief-level material the vault flags as the highest-resonance and hardest to handle, and this post shows how: **address the belief directly and never accuse the reader of holding it.**

**The one-grey/one-black device carries the whole argument.** Every slide is a black clause and a grey clause. The black states, the grey turns. *"Because premium pricing isn't a crime." / "It's a positioning decision."* On the cover it is used to split the sentence so the eye lands on *"charging a higher price"* in grey — the guilty part, set softer.

**Fourteen slides, none longer than about fifteen words.** Same finding as [[30 - Your Attention Span Is Fcked|post 30]]: **slide count is limited by words per slide, not by slides.** This reads in under thirty seconds.

**Slides 2–4 dismantle the guilt in three moves, one per slide.** *Not taking* → *giving a choice* → *nobody is forced.* Each is a rebuttal to an unstated accusation the reader is making against themselves. **Answer the self-accusation before making the case.**

**Slide 9 names the real cost of discounting, and it is psychological rather than financial.** *"You start building a business around your fears. Not your value."* No mention of margin. Compare the vault's why-chain: the answer to "why does it feel wrong to charge more" is not economics, and this post refuses to pretend otherwise.

**Slide 10 is the proof slide and it borrows two universally accepted facts.** Private jet versus economy, Rolex versus digital watch. Neither is about the reader's industry, which is exactly why they work — nobody argues with them, and the principle transfers by itself. **When you have no data, borrow a comparison the reader already believes.**

**Slides 12–13 are the question-swap, split across two slides for timing.** *"The market isn't asking: 'Why are you so expensive?' They're asking: 'Why are you worth it?'"* — then a whole slide for *"And those are two very different questions."* Giving the punchline its own slide forces the pause that makes it land. Same reframe device as [[18 - The Realism Checklist AI Images Fail|post 18]]'s wrong-question/better-question slide.

**The CTA sells a paid product with a comment word.** *"Comment 'BOOK' to purchase my book Think Brand."* Not a free lead magnet — a purchase, initiated by a comment. Notable because every other comment-word funnel in this file gives something away. It works here because the post is fourteen slides of the book's actual thinking, so the reader has already sampled it.

**No images at all, and the account handle plus URL sit on every slide.** For an argument made entirely of sentences, illustration would only dilute. Each slide is also independently screenshot-able with attribution attached — a share-optimised choice.
