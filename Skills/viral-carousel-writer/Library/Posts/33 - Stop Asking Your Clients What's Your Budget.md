## Notes

**The hook attacks a habit, not a belief.** *"Stop asking your clients 'What's your budget?'"* — a specific sentence the reader says out loud in most sales calls. Compare post 31 (attacks a *feeling*: guilt) and post 32 (attacks a *pattern*: dropping everything). **Three posts, three different targets — habit, feeling, pattern — with an identical template.** That is the account's whole content engine.

**Slide 2 does the disarming move in eight words.** *"That sounds normal… but it's a big mistake that is costing you."* It concedes that the reader is behaving reasonably before naming the damage. Without it, slide 1 is an accusation. **Grant the reasonableness of the mistake immediately after naming it** — same function as [[12 - If Claude Writes Your Content|post 12]]'s *"By the way: I LOVE AI"*, compressed into a single clause.

**Slides 3–4 escalate through one causal step.** *"you let the client set your value"* → *"you're not pricing based on results… you're pricing based on their comfort."* The second slide is the one that stings, and it only works because the first established the mechanism. **Never state the worst version first.**

**"Pricing based on their comfort"** is the phrase the whole post exists to deliver. It renames a familiar behaviour into something the reader would never knowingly choose.

**Slides 9–10 are split for rhythm.** *"High-value brands don't ask for budgets."* / *"They define the investment."* Two slides, ten words total. The vault's instinct would be to combine them; keeping them apart forces a swipe between the negative and the positive, which is the beat the contrast needs.

**Slide 12 is the script, again.** *"Based on what you want to achieve, this is the level we recommend and the investment."* Third post in a row where the payoff is one sentence the reader can use verbatim tomorrow. This account's model is consistent: **give away one exact line, sell the thinking behind all of them.**

**Note the word swap is the entire tactic.** *Budget* → *investment*. The post never says "use the word investment" — it demonstrates it across slides 10 and 12 and lets the reader notice. **Showing a vocabulary change is stronger than instructing one.**

**"Here's the better move:"** appears on slide 11 here and slide 13 in post 32 — identical pivot slide, identical function, four words alone on the page. It is a reusable structural component in this account's template, not a one-off.
