## Notes

**Sixteen slides survive because each one is a single sentence and a drawing.** Almost no slide carries more than about twenty words. The vault's 4–9 range assumes slides of paragraph density; this post proves the ceiling is set by **words per slide**, not slide count. A 16-slide post that reads in forty seconds is shorter than a 6-slide post of dense copy.

**The three-tier text pattern is used consistently:** small grey lead-in, large black claim, small grey elaboration. *"You trained it into this." / **"So train it back"** /* — and slide 15 drops the third tier entirely for emphasis. **Varying which tiers are present, while keeping the same grid, is how it gets texture without changing layout.**

**Slides 2–4 build the mechanism as a three-beat causal chain, one beat per slide.** Boredom appeared → you reached → relief arrived in one second. Then the line that closes it: *"Your nervous system wrote that down."* The reader is never blamed; the nervous system is the actor. **Externalising the fault is what keeps a diagnosis post from feeling like an attack.**

**"And relief came in one second flat"** — a specific duration where "quickly" would do. Same instinct as the numbers in the inventory posts, applied to a feeling.

**The prescription slides give physical, checkable instructions.** *"Twenty pages, a walk without headphones, work in a single tab."* Not "practise deep focus". Three concrete objects. Compare [[10 - The 6 Golden Exercises|post 10]]'s *"every bag, box and child you pick up"* — both translate an abstraction into things the reader can picture in their own week.

**Slide 14 is the honesty slide and it is the reason the post is credible.** *"The first two weeks will suck. Withdrawal always does…"* Naming the cost before the reader discovers it is what separates advice from a pitch. Then it resolves into the most precise image in the post: *"one day a book holds you for an hour and you realize the baseline is moving back."*

**"You trained it into this. So train it back."** The whole argument in eight words, placed on the second-to-last slide, in the largest type. Same paired-verdict shape as [[26 - This Is How You Build An Irresistible Brand|post 26]] and [[22 - Why Doing Everything Right Makes You Forgettable|post 22]] — **the strongest line gets its own near-final slide, alone.**

**The hook uses the audience's own register.** *"f*cked"* with the asterisk, then *"here's how to fix it →"* in plain lowercase. The profanity buys the stop; the lowercase second line makes the promise feel casual rather than salesy.

**One illustration style throughout — pencil sketch on white.** Sixteen different drawings, one visual grammar. It is the cheapest possible way to give every slide a distinct image while keeping the post unmistakably one object. Note the drawings are always *the noun of the slide* (a hand, a phone, a pill, a seedling, a pencil), never decoration.

**The CTA is a newsletter, and it is weak by this file's standards.** *"Get my daily field notes of what I'm building, breaking, shipping… (link in bio)."* Off-platform, no comment word, and the three bullets describe the author's activity rather than the reader's benefit. After fifteen slides of precise, useful work, the ask names nothing the reader gets.
