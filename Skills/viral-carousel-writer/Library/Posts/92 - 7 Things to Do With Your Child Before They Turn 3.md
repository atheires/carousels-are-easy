## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**

7 things to do with your child before they turn 3

YOU'LL NEVER GET THESE LITTLE YEARS BACK.

*Watercolour of a father tossing a toddler in the air.*

**2 — page 04**

**3) Let them help you cook**

*Even if* it takes much longer and turns your kitchen upside down.

Let them:
- Stir the bowl
- Pour the ingredients
- Taste the food
- Stand beside you

Right now, they think being with you is the fun part.

*Page number in a circle at the top and an arrow button at the bottom of every slide 04–08.*

**3 — page 05**

**4) Ask them what they want to be when they grow up**

Write down their answer *exactly* the way they say it... even if it makes absolutely no sense.

Ask them:
- Their dream job
- Their favourite animal
- What clouds are made of
- Their biggest adventure

One day, you'll miss the way they saw the world.

**4 — page 06**

**5) Lie down beside them and join their imaginary world**

These little invitations don't last forever.

Play:
- Tea parties
- Superheroes
- Toy cars
- Whatever game they invent

To them, you're not just playing. *You're making childhood magical.*

**5 — page 07**

**6) Trace their little foot on paper**

Write today's date beside it.

Keep:
- Their footprint
- Their handprint
- A favourite drawing
- A little note

It's impossible to imagine how tiny they really are while you're living it.

**6 — page 08**

**7) Ask who their favourite person is... and why**

The reasons children give are often the sweetest things you'll ever hear.

Ask them:
- Who they love most
- What makes them happy
- What makes them laugh
- What they love about you

*You'll want to remember every answer.*

**7 — page 09**

They won't stay this little forever.

The greatest luxury isn't a bigger house.

It isn't a nicer car.

**It's having the time to be there for moments like these.**

**8 — page 10, CTA**

If you're not following us,

this might be **the last time you see us**.

If you are, *welcome*. We're building wealth not just to have more money ...but to have more time for the people who matter most.

Save this and share it with someone who believes that time is life's greatest luxury. 🤎

## Notes

**The cover is a deadline and a promise.** "7 things to do with your child before they turn 3" sets a window, and the subline, "You'll never get these little years back", gives the reason in one sentence. A list post gets its urgency from the age limit.

**Every item has the same four parts.** A bold numbered instruction, one line of permission or reframe ("Even if it takes much longer and turns your kitchen upside down"), a short bullet list of what to do, and one closing sentence that lands the feeling ("One day, you'll miss the way they saw the world."). The last line carries the emotion, so the reader feels it on each slide.

**The tone is permission, not instruction.** "Write down their answer exactly the way they say it... even if it makes absolutely no sense." Every slide takes the pressure off the parent and puts it on the moment.

**The bridge from parenting to the account is one slide.** Slide 09 turns the seven items into a belief ("The greatest luxury isn't a bigger house. It isn't a nicer car. It's having the time to be there"), and the final slide tells the reader that is what the account is for: "We're building wealth not just to have more money ...but to have more time for the people who matter most." The CTA is a save and share, with a blunt follow prompt: "this might be the last time you see us".
