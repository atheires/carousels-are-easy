## Notes

**The cover attacks uncertainty rather than a bad creative habit.** “How to Know / What Content / to Make” meets the reader before any individual idea exists. “Know” promises a way to decide with confidence, which is a different attraction from a list of topics. “Comment ‘Select’ for the full system” already names selection as the commercial subject; the lead magnet is present before the teaching begins.

**Slide 2 gives the whole answer a compact name before asking the reader to learn its parts.** “Use the 70-20-10 Rule.” is immediately followed by “to constantly stay ahead of everyone in your niche...” The three numbers make the method feel bounded and rememberable. The competitive promise adds a reason to care beyond simply filling the calendar, while the ellipsis leaves the mechanism to the next swipe.

**The largest allocation begins with evidence, which lowers the risk of the later experiment.** “70%” goes to “content you know / works based on data.” It comes before either iteration or novelty, establishing that most output should not depend on guessing. “past content of / yours or data from / content outside your / niche” widens the source of proof for a reader who may not yet have enough successful posts of their own.

**The 20% section defines iteration by the size of the change.** “iterations of past / overperforming content” follows the established-content category, which might otherwise leave the reader wondering how the two differ. “You're tweaking one / variable” gives the distinction its usable boundary. “Changing the / hook, the format, or how / it's delivered” then provides three decisions the reader can recognise in the actual work of making a post.

**The main insight is that novelty needs a protected minority share.** “completely new tests” arrives only after 90% of the proposed output has been anchored to evidence or a known winner. “You / have no data to support if it’s / going to work” admits uncertainty before “approaches that nobody in / your space is doing” makes that uncertainty exciting. The competitive promise from slide 2 is thus answered without requiring every post to be unprecedented.

**The chain moves through degrees of knowledge rather than through literal questions.** “you know / works,” “You're tweaking one / variable,” and “You / have no data” progressively loosen certainty. The repeated “of your content should” keeps the three sections comparable while the evidence changes. The reader is silently sorting possible posts into what is established, what is a variation, and what is genuinely untested.

**“New” speeds up the final teaching slide after the careful explanation of iteration.** “New formats, / new ideas, new creative / approaches” supplies three successive starts rather than another technical definition. By contrast, “one / variable” slows the reader down to a controlled adjustment. The emphases on “based on data,” “iterations,” and “completely new tests” hold the distinctions together without adding an extra summary slide.

**This is a Build compressed into allocation rules, with a CTA that raises the desired outcome.** “Comment ‘Select’” ends with “content that gets / you customers,” whereas slide 2 promised to “stay ahead.” The reader has received the proportions and definitions; “the full system” promises the larger process of turning those decisions into commercial results. Repeating that offer on slides 1–3 makes it persistent, and the final customer language gives the request a more concrete reason than access to more content advice alone.
