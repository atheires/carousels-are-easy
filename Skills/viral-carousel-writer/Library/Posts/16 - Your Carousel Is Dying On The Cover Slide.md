## Slide copy, verbatim

**1 — cover**
**Your Carousel is dying** *on the cover slide.*

Here are 5 rules to fix it →

**2 — Rule #1: Your Cover Is Your First Impression**

*❌ / ✅ pair: a busy cover ("5 THINGS YOU SHOULD KNOW ABOUT MAKING BETTER INSTAGRAM CAROUSELS", 01/10, a checklist of "more reach / more likes / more engagement", a "Bonus inside" stamp) against the clean one ("Your Carousel is dying on the cover slide.").*

You have 2 seconds to convince them to swipe.
Most covers try to do too much. Explain everything. Set expectations. Be clever.

**Your cover's only job: create curiosity.**
The brain stops scrolling when there's a question to answer. A cover that creates intrigue gets the swipe.

**3 — Rule #2: Make Your Text Bold, Big & Easy To Scan**

*❌ / ✅ pair: tiny "Trending fonts" label vs. large layered "Trending FONTS".*

If it's small, it fails. If it's hard to read, it fails. If it requires thinking, it fails.

**Can someone read your cover in 0.5 seconds while scrolling at full speed?** If no, make it bigger. If still no, make it bolder.

**Friction kills engagement. Clarity breeds clicks.**

**4 — Rule #3: Make Your Hook Clear & Benefit Driven**

| Bad cover | Good cover |
|---|---|
| "Here are some tips" | "Stop using Pinterest. Here's why" |

**One is information. One is a promise.**

People don't swipe for information. They swipe to solve a problem or satisfy curiosity. **Your cover needs to do one of those two things.**

Benefit-driven hooks speak to the person's immediate need, not the carousel's topic.

**5 — Rule #4: Use Contrast To Make Your Cover Pop**

Color contrast. Text contrast. Visual contrast.
If your cover blends into the feed, it disappears.
If your cover stands out, it stops the scroll.

**The feed is visual noise. High contrast cuts through it.**

**6 — Rule #5: Keep It Clean, Focused & On Brand**

✅ One message. One visual direction. One promise.
❌ Not five things competing for attention.
❌ Not generic stock photos that look like everyone else's.
❌ Not a design that contradicts your brand voice.

**Your cover should feel like it belongs to you. Not borrowed from someone else.**

*Grid of their own covers shown as proof of a consistent look.*

**7 — What most Brands Get Wrong**

**They design the carousel. Then they design a cover to match it. That's backwards.**

The cover should drive the carousel. Not follow it.

**8 — Before You Publish, Ask yourself:**

☐ Does this make someone want to swipe?
☐ Can someone read it in half a second?
☐ Is the benefit immediately clear?
☐ Does it stand out from the feed?
☐ Does it feel like my brand?

If any answer is no, redesign. That's it.

## Notes

**It uses its own cover as the ✅ example on slide 2.** The reader is looking at proof they already responded to — they swiped, so the good example is self-evidently good. Cheapest possible credibility.

**Every rule is a paired image, not a description.** ❌ on the left, ✅ on the right, black cross and green tick. No slide asks the reader to imagine what a bad cover looks like. **Show the failure and the fix side by side rather than describing either.**

**"One is information. One is a promise."** The single most useful line in the file for step 4. *"Here are some tips"* versus *"Stop using Pinterest. Here's why"* — and it maps exactly onto the system's two shapes. Their "promise" is the Promise shape; their "revelation" cover (from post 15) is the Starter shape.

**"They swipe to solve a problem or satisfy curiosity. Your cover needs to do one of those two things."** A cleaner binary than the five-rule checklist the system currently uses — worth testing as a first-pass filter on any headline set.

**The 0.5-second test is more aggressive than the system's current standard.** *"Can someone read your cover in 0.5 seconds while scrolling at full speed? If no, make it bigger. If still no, make it bolder."* Note the fix offered is typographic, not editorial — they assume the words are fine and the size is wrong.

**Slide 7 is the reframe, and it lands late on purpose.** *"They design the carousel. Then they design a cover to match it. That's backwards. The cover should drive the carousel. Not follow it."* This directly contradicts the system's step 8 instruction to **write slide 1 last**. Worth holding both: the system's reasoning is that you cannot promise value you have not built; theirs is that a cover written to fit finished content will always be a description rather than a hook. The resolution is probably that the *idea* of the cover must lead, and its *wording* is finalised last.

**Slide 8 is a five-box pre-publish gate** — the same device as the system's own pre-delivery gate, reduced to five questions and one instruction: *"If any answer is no, redesign. That's it."*

**"Not generic stock photos that look like everyone else's."** Their own covers use styled editorial photography of people mid-gesture, consistently. Note this is the same account whose post 14 used an unrelated swimwear photo — the rule is stated better than it is followed.
