## 1. Two engines, never mixed in one post

Every post is built for reach OR for leads — never both at once. The zero-CTA posts (1, 3, 6, 10, 16) strip out everything that reads as marketing: no comment word, no handle sometimes, no product mention, no hashtags. The CTA posts accept a lower like ceiling in exchange for a comment spike.

This is the single most important structural fact in the dataset, and it maps directly onto your system's "views then leads" rule — she just refuses to chase both in the same asset. **A post decides its job before a word is written.**

## 2. Comment count is manufactured by the gap, not by teaching quality

This is the most load-bearing, most measurable finding across all 16 posts.

- **Post 10** (consistency) is arguably her single best-argued deck — full causal mechanism, six worked examples, nothing withheld, genuine mechanism (dopamine/validation-loop). **65 comments.**
- **Post 16** (failing is sexy) is the most complete post in the set — three named case studies, a full 5-step method, a full metric-reframe table, nothing routed to a DM. **49 comments**, 111:1 like ratio, the worst conversion of the 16.
- **Posts 11 and 12**, same storytelling topic, same design system, withhold one quantified item ("20 prompts," "twenty story angles") behind one comment word. **988 and 1,008 comments.**
- **Post 4** withholds nothing that matters informationally but manufactures a brand-new gap in its last two sentences ("Every year you've been alive is storytelling gold... you just need to know how to mine it") — a gap that didn't exist one slide earlier. **1,903 comments**, the highest in the set.

The pattern: **teach generously, withhold once, and only at the price point — ideally a gap that is manufactured fresh at the very end, not one that's been dangling the whole deck.** A carousel that answers everything gets read, liked, and saved. A carousel that answers everything except one named, quantified thing gets commented on.

Reciprocity compounds this: Post 15 opens with her own confession ("I threw away a successful photography business") and gets 490 comments; Post 16 asks the reader to disclose a personal failure first, discloses none of her own, and gets 49. **She has to go first for the reader to go at all.**

## 3. What she hides is almost always assembly, not information

Across nearly every post: she gives the parts and withholds the sequencing.

- Post 2/5 (7-Beats): all seven beat names, defined, with worked examples — withheld is how to weight/sequence them into one piece of copy.
- Post 4/7 (Storytelling vs Telling Stories): draws the four-station arc, names every station — withheld is how to fill Application, the newest and most valuable station.
- Post 13: gives the full four-bucket sourcing system, operational tonight — withheld is how to pick the one right angle out of the "100 ways" a moment could be told.
- Post 9: nine reframes given in full — withheld is literally everything about the business itself (what she sells, how she got to 7 figures).

She rarely hides *the idea*. She hides *the judgment required to execute it well*. That is a more sophisticated withhold than "here's the what, buy the how" — it lets the free content feel complete while leaving something real behind the paywall.

## 4. Slides lecture, captions confide

Structurally consistent across nearly all 16 posts:
- Slides are near-zero first person. Post 1 has literally none across nine slides. Post 12 has exactly one "I," placed at the CTA. Post 16 has none at all.
- Every hard personal receipt lives in the caption: 100,000 followers lost, ADHD diagnosed at 37, "successful" business burned down at 36, her mother's unused gifts, raising two kids in the absence of a village.
- Her best individual sentences are disproportionately caption-only: "Instagram as a page full of microscopic text that no one can read," "the thousands of people selling the same damn red car," "Your fear can't live in the possibility of failing. It needs to stay in the scenario where you just don't even try."

Slides earn trust through certainty and design. Captions earn it through admission. **Zero hashtags across all 16 posts, without exception** — reach comes entirely from saves, shares, and the comment-word loop, never discovery tags.

## 5. She self-undercuts exactly once per carousel, and it's always the biggest trust purchase in the deck

- Post 6: "yes, plants look good, but don't rely on them to dramatically purify your air" — corrects her own bullet mid-list.
- Post 8: casts open doubt on the "promised land" of entrepreneurship she herself sells, one slide before revealing she sells it.
- Post 5/2: "not about making you a better writer overnight," placed immediately before the ask.
- Post 10/13: "I move my hands a lot and tend to use awkward grammar" — highlighted, on purpose, on the about-me slide.

A carousel that never doubts itself reads as a pitch. One correction, placed anywhere in the middle, converts the whole deck into something that reads as considered rather than performed.

## 6. Typos are left in, systematically, and cost nothing

"is where the trust is build," "doens't," "skills she that helped her," "coverts" for "converts," "Milenial" misspelled on a cover slide, missing question marks, inconsistent quote marks. None of this is cleaned up before publishing. On brand for a creator whose central pitch is "stop filtering every single word" — the roughness is consistent with, and arguably reinforces, the message.

## 7. NESB (New/Easy/Safe/Big) is calibrated to the argument, never maxed by reflex

She does not run all four dials to 10 on every slide. She reads what the post needs:
- **Permission posts** (age, energy, boundaries, motherhood) run 45-70% SAFE. Post 9 ("I'm 37") is ~40% SAFE. Post 15 ("At 36 I changed careers") is 14 of 26 hits SAFE.
- **Mechanism posts** (consistency, storytelling frameworks) lead with NEW, because the whole sale is "this is a genuinely new idea."
- **BIG is almost always withheld from the body and detonated only at the ask.** Post 10 (arguing against outcome-chasing) has almost zero BIG anywhere — a BIG-heavy version would contradict its own thesis. Post 16's BIG is entirely borrowed (Blakely/Oprah/Wolfe Herd's billions), never claimed for herself, because the post isn't selling anything.
- **EASY is always "you already have the material."** Ten seconds before a decision, doing the dishes, your Tuesday, a coffee spill, one step a day. The bar is never "learn something," it's "notice something you already did today."

## 8. Hooks are de-risked by a hedge, never a flat superlative

"(almost) Anything," "I Hardly See Anyone Using" (hardly, not never), "Disgustingly Skilled at Boring Storytelling" (the paradox is the hedge), "If You're in Your 30s or 40s, Read This:" (a colon, not a claim). She almost never writes an unqualified promise. The qualifier is what makes the promise survive scrutiny and feel like it's coming from a real, slightly self-aware person rather than a marketer.

## 9. Definition-by-negation and antithesis are her default teaching devices

"Not X. It's Y." appears constantly across every post: "It's not magic. It's biology." / "Not behind-the-scenes." / "Stop seeing failure as judgment. Start seeing failure as proof." / "You're not starting over. You're starting from wisdom." She teaches by drawing the wrong answer first and crossing it out, not by defining the right one cold. Paired with parallel antithesis as her signature closing rhythm on nearly every slide: two clauses, mirrored grammar, opposite payload.

## 10. The comment word is always the reader's own conclusion, never a brand name

"VOICE" is seeded on the slide before it's asked for ("You're starting from wisdom" → comment WISDOM). "ARC" is the exact concept the diagram three slides earlier made the reader want. "STORYLINE" is what the reader now believes their ordinary life needs help finding. Typing the word is agreement, not compliance — which is why comment rates on her CTA posts run 8-16% rather than the low single digits typical of a generic "comment X for the link."

## Practical implications for the system here

- **Decide a post's job (reach or lead) before writing it**, and let that decision govern whether any CTA appears at all — not just what the CTA says.
- When there is an offer: **give everything except the assembly/judgment layer**, and manufacture the final gap fresh in the last slide or two rather than dangling one gap the whole way through.
- **Reserve first person and hard receipts for the caption.** Let slides run declarative and third/second person; let the caption be where she admits, confesses, or discloses.
- **One self-correction per carousel**, placed mid-deck, buys more trust than any amount of certainty.
- Calibrate NESB to the argument's actual shape — don't reach for BIG reflexively; a post arguing against outcome-chasing should barely use it.
- If asking the reader to disclose something vulnerable, **disclose first.**
